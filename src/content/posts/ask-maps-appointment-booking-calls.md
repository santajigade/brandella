Ask Maps appointment booking is now rolling out in Google Maps. Google's conversational assistant can look for open slots at beauty, pet care, auto and home repair businesses, check availability online and even phone local businesses on the user's behalf. It then shows the open times so the user can pick one.

Google announced the update on November 16, 2026 in a post titled [New Ask Maps features to help with holiday travel and prep](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-holiday-travel-features-2026/), written by Maz Khorasani, Product Director for Google Maps. Appointment finding launches first in the US, on Android and iOS.

For salons, groomers, garages and home service pros, this is a real shift. A customer may never visit your website or call you directly. Instead, an AI assistant may call your front desk, ask about a 4 p.m. slot and report back.

![Infographic comparing the four new Ask Maps features and where they are available](/images/infographics/ig-ask-maps-appointment-booking-calls-1.webp)

## What Google announced for Ask Maps

The November 16 post lists four new features. Here is what each one does and where it is available, based on Google's announcement.

| Feature | What it does | Availability |
|---|---|---|
| Appointment finding | Finds beauty, pet care, auto and home repair slots online and calls businesses for open times | US first |
| "Know before you go" route tips | Shows parking, EV charging, weather, delays and tolls with directions | US first |
| Road trip itineraries | Builds day by day plans with numbered pins, shareable or exportable to Google Docs on desktop | All Ask Maps markets |
| Saved list search | Checks live availability across places in your saved lists for a group size and time | All Ask Maps markets |

Google's example request for appointments is "Find me a 4 p.m. nail appointment near me." The post says Ask Maps will show open slots across nearby businesses so it is easy to decide what to book. The user still chooses the business.

Google also said Ask Maps is expanding to 30 European countries, including Spain, Germany and the UK, which takes it to more than 200 countries and territories. A separate Google post, [Ask Maps is coming to Europe](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/maps/ask-maps-is-coming-to-europe/), says the assistant draws on information about more than 300 million places and reviews from over 500 million contributors.

## How Ask Maps appointment booking works

The feature uses what Google calls new agentic capabilities. In plain terms, the AI does the legwork a customer would normally do: it checks online booking pages and, where needed, calls the business to ask about availability.

[Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/phones/google-maps-can-now-organize-multi-day-trips-and-call-businesses-to-check-appointments/) describes the same flow: Ask Maps checks online availability or calls businesses, then shows the available slots. The final booking choice stays with the user.

Google has not explained several things that matter to business owners:

- How a business can opt out of AI calls or manage them.
- Whether Ask Maps completes the booking or only reports open times.
- Whether any fees or ads are involved.

[PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-maps-ai-now-calls-us-salons-groomers-and-garages-to-find-open-slots/) also notes that these points are not covered in the announcement. Until Google publishes help documentation, treat them as open questions.

## What local businesses should do now

These are Brandella's recommendations, not official Google guidance.

![Checklist of six steps local businesses can take to prepare for Ask Maps appointment calls](/images/infographics/ig-ask-maps-appointment-booking-calls-2.webp)

1. **Audit your Business Profile today.** Check hours, holiday hours, services, categories and phone number. Wrong hours mean the AI may call when you are closed.
2. **Make online booking easy to read.** If you use a booking tool, link it from your profile and keep real availability in it. An AI that can see slots online may not need to call.
3. **Brief your front desk.** Tell staff that some calls may come from an automated assistant asking about open times. Give them a short script with your next available slots.
4. **Answer the phone in peak hours.** If calls go unanswered, the assistant may simply show a competitor's slots instead.
5. **List services clearly.** Use plain service names such as "gel manicure" or "brake inspection" so requests match what you offer.
6. **Track call and booking sources.** Note new booking patterns over the holiday season so you can spot traffic from Ask Maps.

If you are setting up a profile from scratch, start with our [Google Business Profile guide for beginners](/google-my-business-profile-complete-guide-beginners/).

## Who is affected first

The first wave covers US businesses in four groups: beauty (salons, nail and hair studios), pet care (groomers and similar services), auto repair and home repair. Google says more regions are coming soon for appointment finding and route tips, but gave no dates.

Businesses outside the US should still prepare. Itineraries and saved list search are already live wherever Ask Maps works, and those features also lean on accurate hours and availability.

## FAQ about Ask Maps appointment booking

### Does Ask Maps book the appointment for the customer?

Google says Ask Maps shows open slots across nearby businesses so the user can decide what to book. The announcement does not say the assistant completes the booking itself.

### Can a business stop Ask Maps from calling?

Google has not published any opt out or call settings for businesses so far. Watch Google Business Profile help pages for updates.

### Which countries get appointment finding?

Appointment finding and route tips launch first in the US. Google says more regions are coming soon.

### Do I need a booking system to be included?

Google has not said. Ask Maps can check online availability or call the business, so a phone number and accurate hours matter either way.
