<h2 data-start="902" data-end="1112" id="google-ai-search-opt-out-introduction">Google AI Search Opt Out: Introduction</h2><p data-start="902" data-end="1112">If you run a website, blog, or <a href="/category/digital-marketing/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">digital </a>business, you probably depend on Google for traffic.</p><p data-start="902" data-end="1112"> </p><p data-start="902" data-end="1112">Now imagine Google uses your content to create AI-generated summaries in search results but you don’t get the click.</p><p data-start="902" data-end="1112"> </p><p data-start="1114" data-end="1171">That is exactly where the current discussion is happening.</p><p data-start="1114" data-end="1171"> </p><p data-start="1173" data-end="1364">Google has said it is exploring the idea of allowing websites to opt out of AI search features specifically. Not from search completely. Just from AI-generated summaries and similar features.</p><p data-start="1173" data-end="1364"> </p><p data-start="1366" data-end="1407">Let us understand this</p>
<h2 data-start="1414" data-end="1435" id="what-is-happening">What Is Happening?</h2><p data-start="1437" data-end="1580">Google has introduced <a href="https://search.google/intl/en-IN/ways-to-search/ai-overviews/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AI-powered search features</a>. These include AI-generated summaries at the top of search results, often called AI Overviews.<br /><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />These summaries pull information from different websites and present answers directly in Google search.<br /></span><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />For users, it feels helpful and fast.<br /></span><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />But for publishers and website owners, it creates a big question:<br /></span><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />If Google gives the answer directly, will users still click on my website?<br /></span><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />Now Google says it is exploring updates that may allow websites to opt out of these AI features separately.<br /></span><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />Notice the word “exploring.”<br /></span><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />There is no timeline. No confirmed feature. Just exploration.<br /></span><span style="letter-spacing: 0px;"><br />But even that is important.</span></p>
<h2 data-start="2105" data-end="2146" id="the-current-problem-for-website-owners">The Current Problem for Website Owners</h2><p data-start="2148" data-end="2234">Right now, Google provides some control options.
