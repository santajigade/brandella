Google has published a Google Search ranking hub, a new section of its Search Central documentation that explains how it ranks results, how it announces changes and how site owners can push back. The [documentation changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates) lists the change on November 24, 2026 as "Added a ranking section to the Search Central documentation."

The [hub page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/ranking) calls itself the "central place to learn how Google ranks search results and to keep up with changes to our ranking systems." It arrived on the same day the November 2026 core update finished and Google preannounced a December core update.

If you report on organic traffic, these pages are worth reading. Google now says plainly how it treats AI results, where it posts notice of updates and what feedback from site owners can and cannot do.

![Google Search ranking hub at a glance: 3 new pages added November 24, 2026, 3 ranking principles, feedback through Search Console](/images/infographics/ig-google-search-ranking-hub-1.webp)

## What is in the Google Search ranking hub

The ranking section has three new pages, plus a link to the existing ranking systems guide:

| Page | What it covers |
|---|---|
| [Our principles for ranking results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/ranking/principles-for-ranking-results) | How Google says it decides what ranks, and how it tests changes |
| [How to stay informed on ranking updates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/ranking/stay-informed) | Where Google gives notice of core updates and policy changes |
| [Submit feedback about ranking changes](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/ranking/submit-feedback) | How site owners send feedback or ask for dispute resolution |

The hub also links to Google's "Debug traffic drops" guide and the Search Central Community forum for troubleshooting.

## Google's three ranking principles

Google lists three principles: "Users come first", "Consistent systems across all types of results" and "Rigorous testing." The hub says Google ranks all result types "based on their usefulness to the user's query", does not design its systems or AI models to favour any site's interests, and "never accept[s] payment" to rank pages higher in organic results.

The principles page says Google prioritises "helpful, reliable information" made to benefit people. Results now include "images, videos, charts, maps, AI experiences", and Google says web listings, AI Overviews, carousels, images and videos "are evaluated using the same criteria."

Google also says it does not make "manual adjustments to alter search rankings" except to meet legal obligations or apply its Content Policies. On testing, it measures changes by the helpfulness of the whole results page, and states: "if we can't show a change actually makes things better for people, we don't launch it."

None of this is a new ranking factor, so teams already working from Google's people first guidance and [E-E-A-T](/eeat-experience-expertise-authority-trust/) do not need to change direction. What is new is Google saying in writing that AI Overviews are judged by the same criteria as blue links.

## How Google will give notice of ranking updates

The stay informed page says Google gives notice of "actionable policy changes" and "significant ranking changes" such as core updates. The main record is the [Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/products/rGHU1u87FJnkP6W2GwMi/history), which has an Atom feed. Google also posts on the Search Central Blog and the documentation changelog.

For the UK, notice follows the Competition and Markets Authority's fair ranking conduct requirements for Google. The page also gives timing advice. Wait until an update is marked complete "plus at least a week of post-rollout data" before you judge traffic changes. That applies to the [November 2026 core update](/google-november-2026-core-update/) that has just finished.

## The new ranking feedback route in Search Console

When Google rolls out a significant ranking change or actionable policy update that appears on the Search Status Dashboard, verified site owners can click Submit Feedback in Google Search Console. Google's example tag is "#NovemberCoreUpdate2026".

The page suggests including:

- A snapshot of the performance report
- The exact queries, country and language
- Example URLs, including competing URLs that "appear to rank inappropriately"
- The impact, such as a screenshot of the traffic change
- Before and after data for context

"Engineering teams review submissions in aggregate to identify systemic issues," the page says, and "Submitting feedback doesn't result in manual ranking adjustments for individual URLs."

The same page lists dispute routes by type: manual action reconsideration, counterfeit goods appeals, SafeSearch reconsideration, DMCA counter notices, EU Digital Markets Act appeals and a UK CMA indexing appeal that Google says opens on December 17, 2026.

## What SEO teams should do now

These steps are our recommendations, based on the new pages:

1. Subscribe to the Search Status Dashboard Atom feed so update notices reach your team the day they post.
2. Mark November 24, 2026 as the end of the November core update in your reports, then wait at least a week before you judge its impact.
3. If a core update hurt you and you think it is wrong, prepare feedback with exact queries, URLs and dates from the [Search Console performance report](/search-console-performance-report/), and remember it will not trigger a manual fix.
4. Bookmark the principles page as a reference for stakeholders who ask whether Google ranks AI results differently.
5. Check the new generative AI controls page published the same day, which explains how Google uses content for AI training and grounding.

## FAQ about the Google Search ranking hub

### When did Google launch the Search ranking hub?

Google added the ranking section to Search Central on November 24, 2026, according to its documentation changelog.

### Can feedback in Search Console restore my rankings?

No. Google says submissions are reviewed in aggregate to find systemic issues and do not lead to manual ranking adjustments for individual URLs.

### Does Google rank AI Overviews differently from web results?

Google says web listings, AI Overviews, carousels, images and videos are evaluated using the same criteria.

### Where does Google announce core updates?

On the Search Status Dashboard, with the Search Central Blog and the documentation changelog as extra channels.
