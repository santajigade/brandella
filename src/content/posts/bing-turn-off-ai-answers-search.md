Microsoft now gives searchers two ways to turn off AI answers in Bing: a preview browser extension called Microsoft Bing AI Search Choice, and a simple -ai operator added to the end of a query. Both return Bing results without AI generated answers.

The options were set out in a Bing blog post titled [Your choice: Turn AI answers on or off in Bing](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/11/your-choice-turn-ai-answers-on-or-off-in-bing/), dated November 11, 2026. The post says plainly that "Bing users should have a choice in how they search." [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/bing-turn-off-ai-search-setting-42520.html) reported that Bing's Jordi Ribas reshared the news on X, saying there had been much interest in the feature.

For SEO and content teams this is a small product change with a useful side effect. It gives you an easy way to see your Bing rankings with and without the AI layer, and it is a reminder that some searchers will deliberately choose the classic results page.

![Two ways to turn off AI answers in Bing compared](/images/infographics/ig-bing-turn-off-ai-answers-search-1.webp)

## The two ways to turn off AI answers in Bing

**1. The Microsoft Bing AI Search Choice extension (preview).** According to Bing, the extension is available for Google Chrome and Microsoft Edge. It adds a toggle that switches AI generated answers on or off, and the setting applies to all Bing searches. Installing it also makes Bing the default search engine in that browser. Because it is a preview, Microsoft says its features may change based on feedback.

**2. The -ai operator.** No install is needed. Add -ai to any Bing query and Bing returns results without AI generated answers. Bing's own example is searching "why is the sky blue -ai" instead of "why is the sky blue".

| | AI Search Choice extension | -ai operator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setup | Install in Chrome or Edge | None |
| Scope | All Bing searches in that browser | One query at a time |
| Side effect | Sets Bing as default search engine | None |
| Status | Preview, may change | Available now |

## What is missing: a normal search setting

Neither option is a regular switch inside Bing's own search settings. Search Engine Roundtable points out that Bing previously offered a setting to disable Copilot responses and later removed it, and its author questioned why the choice is not back in settings.

That matters for how many people will actually use it. A per query operator and a browser extension are power user tools. Most searchers will keep seeing AI answers by default, so do not expect a big shift in how Bing traffic behaves overnight.

## What this means for SEO on Bing

For most sites Bing sends less traffic than Google, but in our view it is still a channel worth tracking on its own. Treat this change as a measurement opportunity rather than a traffic event.

- **You can now compare both layouts cleanly.** Run the same query with and without -ai and you see the classic blue links view next to the AI view. That shows whether your page is visible in one, both or neither.
- **AI answers and rankings are different jobs.** A page can rank well in classic results and still be skipped in the AI answer, or the reverse. Our guide on [how to increase AI search citations](/how-to-increase-ai-search-citations/) covers the content side of earning those mentions.
- **Choice cuts both ways.** Searchers who opt out of AI see more traditional results, which can help sites that lose clicks to AI summaries. We looked at that click pressure in [AI Overviews are eating your clicks](/ai-overviews-clicks-how-to-get-cited-2026/).

## How this fits Bing's publisher controls

This is the user side of choice. Microsoft has also been working on the publisher side. In an October 30, 2026 post, [Your content, your choice](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/10/your-content-your-choice-clearer-preferences-for-training-and-grounding/), Bing explained new separate preferences for AI training and AI grounding.

According to that post, training can be blocked site wide in robots.txt or per page with a meta tag or X-Robots-Tag header, while grounding is set at page level with Allow, DisallowExtended or Disallow. Bing says the new preferences take effect from February 1, 2027 as it crawls, and sites have until March 1, 2027 to publish them. If you have not reviewed those settings yet, this is a good week to do it alongside your [robots.txt setup](/robots-txt-complete-guide/).

## What SEO teams should do now

![What SEO teams should do now about Bing AI answers](/images/infographics/ig-bing-turn-off-ai-answers-search-2.webp)

These steps are Brandella recommendations, not official Microsoft guidance.

1. **Pick 20 money queries.** Choose the Bing queries that bring the most leads or sales, using the query report in [Bing Webmaster Tools](/bing-webmaster-tools-complete-guide/).
2. **Check each one twice.** Search it normally and again with -ai. Note whether your page appears in the AI answer, in the classic results, or both.
3. **Log the gaps.** Where you rank in classic results but are missing from the AI answer, look at whether the page answers the question directly in the first lines and has clear headings.
4. **Recheck monthly.** AI answers change often. A simple spreadsheet with date, query, AI answer cited yes or no, and classic position is enough.
5. **Review your AI preferences.** Decide whether you want to allow training and grounding, then plan the robots.txt and meta tag changes before the March 1, 2027 deadline Bing has set.
6. **Do not change your Bing strategy yet.** Opt out tools that need an operator or an extension will reach a minority of users. Track the data before you move budget or content priorities.

## The bigger picture for AI search choice

Search engines are under pressure from users who want classic results and from publishers who want control over how their content feeds AI. Bing's answer, for now, is to offer choice through add ons rather than a default switch.

For marketers the practical takeaway is simple. Measure visibility in both the AI layer and the classic results, keep your content easy to quote, and set your AI preferences on purpose rather than by default.
