The second Google Ads Creative Drop is out, and it is aimed squarely at the holiday rush. In a post titled [Inside Creative Drop #2](https://business.google.com/us/accelerate/announcements/optimize-holiday-ads-with-google-ai-creative-drop-2/), published in early November 2026 on Google's Accelerate with Google site, the Google Ads product teams walk through three AI features: campaign translation, festive image edits in Asset Studio and video made with Gemini Omni 1.2.

Creative Drop is a monthly series, and this edition is less about brand new products and more about how to use them for seasonal campaigns. Each feature comes with a tip from the product manager behind it, which is the most useful part for advertisers.

If you run Search, YouTube or Performance Max campaigns and want to scale creative or reach new language markets before the end of the year, here is what changed and how to test it safely.

## What is in Google Ads Creative Drop 2

Google's post covers three tools. The table below sums up what each does, based on Google's own descriptions.

| Feature | What Google says it does | Product manager tip |
|---|---|---|
| AI Campaign Translation | Localize, review and launch campaigns inside Google Ads, in 15 languages | Review translated campaigns while they are paused |
| Multimodal Asset Studio | Turn existing product photos into festive scenes, without a new shoot | Upload brand guidelines and four to five product angles |
| Gemini Omni 1.2 video | Create horizontal, vertical and square videos for YouTube and Google campaigns | A/B test two different opening hooks |

Google's page does not give a rollout schedule, a list of eligible countries or a beta label for these features. It points advertisers to [Asset Studio in Google Ads](https://ads.google.com/aw/assetstudio/) to get started, so check your own account before you plan around any of them.

## AI campaign translation now covers 15 languages

The headline feature for international advertisers is translation. Google says you can localize, review and launch campaigns without exporting spreadsheets or using separate translation tools. It names Spanish, French, German and Portuguese as examples but does not list all 15 languages.

This builds on the AI campaign localization tool for Search campaigns that Google released as a beta earlier this autumn. [PPC Land reported in October](https://ppc.land/google-ads-ai-tool-copies-search-campaigns-into-10-new-languages/) that the help page at the time supported English (US) as the source and 10 target languages, created a brand new campaign while leaving the original untouched, published new campaigns paused and copied budgets without any currency conversion. Google had said more languages would come by the end of 2026, which fits the new figure of 15, though Google's post does not spell out the link.

Product manager Mindy Lin's advice is simple: review translated campaigns while they are still paused, so you can adjust the brand tone before anything goes live.

## Festive product images in Asset Studio

The second feature turns everyday product photos into holiday scenes, such as winter or gift themed settings, without a new photoshoot. Google says the goal is to keep the results brand compliant.

Product manager Charles Boyd shares three practical tips. Import a brand guidelines PDF so the tool knows your colors, fonts and keyword rules. Supply four to five angles of the product. And describe seasonal lighting in the prompt, for example warm holiday table lighting or a snowy outdoor backdrop.

## Gemini Omni 1.2 video for YouTube

The third tool is video. Google describes Gemini Omni 1.2 as an updated video generation model from Google DeepMind, available through multimodal video creation in Asset Studio, with stronger consistency and faster creation at scale. It can produce horizontal, vertical and square formats.

We covered the model in detail in our post on [Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash and 15 second AI video](/gemini-omni-1-2-flash-video-for-marketers/). The new piece here is the testing advice from product manager Max Hagler: run an A/B test comparing two different visual hooks in the first three seconds, using both vertical and horizontal versions.

## What advertisers should do now

These steps are Brandella's recommendations for using the Creative Drop 2 tools without risking peak season performance.

1. **Check access first.** Open Asset Studio and the campaign tools in your account and confirm which features you actually see. Google has not published availability details.
2. **Translate one campaign, not all of them.** Pick a single well performing Search campaign and a single new market. Keep the translated copy paused until a native speaker has read every headline, description and keyword.
3. **Fix budgets and landing pages before launch.** If the tool copies budgets without converting currency, as PPC Land reported, set the right amount for the new market yourself. Make sure the landing page is in the same language, and if you run separate language versions of your site, review our guide to [hreflang tags](/hreflang-tags-explained/).
4. **Prepare your brand kit.** Gather a guidelines PDF, logo files and four to five clean product angles before you open Asset Studio. Better inputs mean fewer off brand images to reject.
5. **Label AI creative where required.** Google has been expanding disclosure for AI generated ads, as we covered in [Google Ads AI labels expanding to more regions](/google-ads-ai-labels-expand-california-korea-vietnam/). Check whether your markets require it.
6. **Test hooks, not just formats.** Follow Google's own tip and compare two opening hooks. Use the same structured approach you would use for [PPC ad copy testing](/ppc-ad-copy-testing/): one variable at a time, enough budget and a clear success metric.
7. **Avoid big changes during peak days.** Launch tests early in the season so learning finishes before your biggest sales days, not during them.

## Who benefits most

Brands selling into several countries gain the most from in platform translation, because it removes a slow export and import step. Retailers with large catalogs but small creative budgets get the most from festive image edits. And YouTube and [Demand Gen advertisers](/demand-gen-campaigns-explained/) who struggle to produce enough video versions can use Omni to fill format gaps.

Whatever you test, treat AI output as a first draft. The tools can save hours, but a human check on language, tone and claims is still the step that protects your brand during the busiest weeks of the year.
