Instagram content protection is now rolling out to eligible creators. Instagram announced the tool on its [creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/introducing-content-protection/) on November 17, 2026. It scans Instagram and Facebook for copies of a creator's original videos and lets the creator decide what happens to each copy.

The feature is not brand new at Meta. [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-content-protection-creators/) reports that Meta launched it on Facebook in November 2025, and it uses the same matching technology as Meta's Rights Manager. What is new is Instagram access and a planned test that targets AI made copies.

For creators, brands and social media managers, this changes the response to stolen Reels. Instead of reporting copies one by one, enrolled accounts can get alerts and act from one place.

![Infographic showing the four options for an Instagram content protection match](/images/infographics/ig-instagram-content-protection-reposts-ai-1.webp)

## How Instagram content protection works

Once a creator enrolls, every original video they post to Instagram is protected automatically, according to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/instagrams-new-tool-for-creators-targets-unauthorized-reposts-and-ai-modified-videos/). Older eligible videos can be added by selecting them manually.

Instagram then keeps scanning for matches. When another account's Reel matches a protected video, the creator gets an alert. Net Influencer says the alert shows the copy's view count and the follower count of the profile that posted it, which helps decide whether a copy is worth acting on.

Creators can also add an allow list. Accounts they have authorized, such as a second profile, a collaborator or a brand partner, will not be flagged.

## The four choices for every match

Each match gives the creator four options, as described by Net Influencer and [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/instagram-content-protection-stolen-videos-ai-jokes/).

| Option | What happens |
|---|---|
| Track | The copy stays visible and you monitor it. This is the default. |
| Hide from everyone | The copy can no longer be viewed on Instagram or Facebook. |
| Add credit link | An "original by" label on the copy links viewers to your original. |
| Release the match | You drop the claim and the video stays up. |

On hiding, Instagram says it reduces the copy's reach "without directly resulting in a penalty to the other profile," per Net Influencer. So hiding is about your video, not about punishing the account.

If another profile protects your video first, Net Influencer says you can file an ownership dispute in the tool or a copyright report through Instagram's Intellectual Property Reporting Center.

## Who can use it

Instagram says eligibility is "largely based on meeting our originality and integrity standards." Eligible creators see a notification in their activity feed, and access sits under "Your professional tools" in the Tools section, according to Net Influencer. Creators who were not notified can apply.

None of the reports we read say which countries are included. If you do not see the tool yet, check the professional tools area and apply rather than assuming it is not available.

## The AI copy test

The second part of the news targets a growing problem: videos that keep a creator's words but swap the person for an AI version. TechCrunch reports that the tool will start detecting AI generated videos that reuse audio from a creator's original, even when the visuals are changed.

This begins with a small group of creators "in the coming weeks," then expands to creators enrolled on both Instagram and Facebook. Instagram is also exploring ways to limit the reach of accounts that repeatedly use AI tools to repost other people's work. That fits a wider trend we have seen with [LinkedIn demoting AI generated comments](/linkedin-ai-generated-comments-demoted/).

## What creators and brands should do now

These steps are Brandella's recommendations, not official Instagram guidance.

![Infographic with five steps to set up Instagram content protection](/images/infographics/ig-instagram-content-protection-reposts-ai-2.webp)

- **Check your notifications and tools.** Look for the invite in your activity feed or under Your professional tools. Apply if it is missing.
- **Protect your best back catalogue.** After enrolling, manually add your top performing older Reels, since those are the ones most likely to be copied.
- **Set up the allow list first.** Add your second profile, agency accounts and brand partners so paid or approved reposts are not flagged.
- **Use credit links for big copies.** If a large account reposts your video, an "original by" label can send its audience to you instead of just hiding the copy.
- **Agree a policy with brands.** Put reuse rights and allow list steps in creator contracts, so a campaign repost does not trigger an alert.
- **Keep posting original work.** Eligibility depends on originality standards. Our [Instagram marketing guide](/instagram-marketing-guide-businesses/) covers formats that build a strong original presence.

Brands that run several Facebook and Instagram identities should also review recent account changes, such as [Facebook removing additional profiles](/facebook-additional-profiles-removed/), before adding accounts to an allow list.

## FAQ about Instagram content protection

### Is Instagram content protection free?

None of the reports we read mention a fee. Access depends on eligibility, which Instagram says is largely based on its originality and integrity standards.

### Does hiding a copy penalize the other account?

Instagram says hiding reduces the copy's reach "without directly resulting in a penalty to the other profile," according to Net Influencer.

### Does it catch AI versions of my videos?

Not yet for everyone. A test that finds videos reusing your spoken words with AI changed visuals starts with a small group of creators in the coming weeks.

### Can I protect videos I posted before enrolling?

Yes. Eligible older videos can be added manually after you enroll.
