The LinkedIn Frequent Viewer tag is a new test in the "Who viewed your profile" list. The tag marks people who have looked at your profile more than once, and it is showing for some Premium subscribers. LinkedIn confirmed the test to [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/linkedin-is-testing-a-feature-that-lets-you-see-your-profile-stalkers/), which reported it on November 11, 2026, and [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-tests-frequent-viewer-feature/874463/) covered it the same day.

For B2B marketers, founders and sales teams, a repeat profile visit is one of the clearest interest signals LinkedIn shows. Someone who comes back to your profile three times in a week is more likely to be weighing a call, a hire or a deal than someone who scrolled past once.

It is a test, though, and many of the details are still unknown. Here is what has been reported, what is not confirmed, and how to use the signal without crossing into creepy territory.

![LinkedIn Frequent Viewer test: what is confirmed and what is still unknown](/images/infographics/ig-linkedin-frequent-viewer-tag-test-1.webp)

## What the LinkedIn Frequent Viewer tag shows

Based on WIRED's reporting as summarised by [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-tests-a-label-for-the-profile-visitors-who-keep-coming-back/), the tag appears next to people who have repeatedly viewed a member's profile. Viewing your posts or seeing you in search results does not trigger it, so it reflects actual profile visits.

The two reports describe it slightly differently. WIRED describes a tag, while Social Media Today describes a visit count per profile. Until LinkedIn publishes help documentation, it is not clear which version users will see, or whether both are being tested.

## What LinkedIn has not confirmed

LinkedIn confirmed the test but, according to PPC Land, has not disclosed several key details:

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Is the test live? | Yes, confirmed by LinkedIn to WIRED |
| Who sees it? | Some Premium subscribers, not all |
| How many visits trigger the tag? | Not disclosed |
| Over what time window? | Not disclosed |
| Which markets and how big is the test? | Not disclosed |
| Does it appear in Sales Navigator? | Not disclosed |

PPC Land reports that Krista Mollion, founder of Unignorable Brands, first noticed the tag in October 2026. She sorts her frequent viewers into friends, people likely to pitch her, and possible sales or recruiting leads, and says this has led to useful conversations.

## Privacy: who can be seen and who cannot

The tag only applies to visitors LinkedIn already shows you. LinkedIn members can choose to view profiles in public, semi-private or private mode, and PPC Land notes that private mode turns off the viewer feature for the visitor themselves. So people who browse privately will not appear, frequent or not.

Critics have pointed out that the tag could reveal unwanted watchers, such as a former partner or a former boss. PPC Land also notes the test comes after a May 2026 GDPR complaint by the privacy group noyb to the Austrian data protection authority about LinkedIn charging for profile visitor data.

If you prospect on LinkedIn yourself, assume your repeat visits may become more visible. Decide whether you browse in public or private mode based on what you are comfortable with.

## How B2B marketers can use repeat profile visits

![Steps for B2B marketers to use LinkedIn Frequent Viewer signals](/images/infographics/ig-linkedin-frequent-viewer-tag-test-2.webp)

These are Brandella recommendations, not LinkedIn guidance. Whether or not you have the tag yet, the approach works with the normal profile viewer list too.

1. **Check your profile viewers weekly.** Look for names that appear more than once, and note their company and role. A repeat visitor from a target account is worth a closer look.
2. **Sort before you reach out.** Split repeat viewers into groups such as prospects, partners, recruiters, peers and people likely to pitch you. Only the first two usually need action.
3. **Warm up before you message.** Engage with their recent posts or company page first. A comment that adds value is a softer start than a cold message.
4. **Never open with "I saw you viewing my profile."** It puts people on the defensive. Lead with something useful that matches their role instead.
5. **Make the visit worth it.** Repeat visitors are reading your headline, About section and featured posts. Keep them clear and current, as covered in our [LinkedIn marketing strategy guide](/linkedin-marketing-strategy/).
6. **Track outcomes.** Log which repeat viewers turned into calls or deals so you know whether the signal is worth your time.

## Why profile signals matter more now

LinkedIn has been changing how profiles and posts are matched. Its feed now uses your profile to judge relevance, which we explained in [LinkedIn profile relevance](/linkedin-profile-relevance-feed-llm/). A stronger profile helps you appear to the right people, and a repeat viewer signal helps you see which of them are paying attention.

Company pages matter too. If your team gets frequent viewers from the same organisation, send them to useful content on your [LinkedIn company page](/grow-your-linkedin-company-page/) and keep the message consistent.

## What to watch next

Because this is a test, the tag could change, expand to more Premium members or disappear. Watch for an official LinkedIn help page that explains the visit threshold, time window and markets. Until then, treat the Frequent Viewer tag as a helpful hint for outreach timing, not a lead score you can rely on.
