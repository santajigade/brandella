The Meta commercial terms consultation now has a shape and a rough timeline. In a newsroom post titled [Our Approach to Updating Our Commercial Terms of Service](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/our-approach-to-updating-our-commercial-terms-of-service/), published on November 18, 2026, Meta set out three phases for revising the terms it paused earlier this month. The last phase starts in early 2027.

We covered the pause itself in [Meta Commercial Terms Paused: What It Means for Your Website](/meta-commercial-terms-paused-crawling/). The new post adds the process. It still gives no new effective date, and the existing terms stay in force.

This affects anyone who runs ads with Meta or links a website to a Meta Business account, and news publishers most of all. Meta says it made the decision "after hearing concerns from news publishers and other stakeholders about how the proposed updates apply to our web crawlers."

![Meta commercial terms consultation in three phases, from now to early 2027](/images/infographics/ig-meta-commercial-terms-consultation-2027-1.webp)

## The three phases of the Meta commercial terms consultation

Meta's post lists the phases in order. Only the third has a date attached.

| Phase | What Meta says it will do | Timing given |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Listening to stakeholders | Briefings and feedback talks with news publishers across markets and global industry groups, already started | "Over the next few months" |
| 2. Considering feedback | Revise the terms and refine the language it first previewed | None given |
| 3. Further engagement before rollout | Engage news publishers, other stakeholders and regulators on the updated terms, and publish an explainer document | Early 2027 |

Meta says that in the first round of briefings it explained how its crawlers work and "the potential products that future changes to our terms seek to enable." The post does not name those products. Overall, Meta says, "this phased approach may take a few months to complete."

## What has not changed

The terms in force today are still the existing ones. Meta's November 5 Business Help Center notice, [as reported by Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/11/meta-pulls-down-controversial-terms-of-service/), said the updates to the Commercial Terms, Product Catalog Terms, Self-Serve Ad Terms and Ad Creative Generative AI Terms "are now paused and will not take effect on November 20, 2026," and that "our existing terms remain in effect."

The November 18 post adds one commitment for account holders. "As we typically do, we will notify account holders ahead of the new terms going into effect," Meta writes, with a period of review. It does not say how long that period will be.

On crawling, Meta's only statement is this: "Meta follows the industry standard robots.txt mechanism as described in our web crawler developer doc." The post does not restate what the original proposed wording allowed.

## Why the timeline moved

The pause followed weeks of pushback. According to [PPC Land's November 22 report](https://ppc.land/metas-website-crawling-terms-stay-shelved-into-2027-as-publisher-talks-run/), an Oslo court issued an interim order on October 28 for 10 Norwegian media groups, the UK Competition and Markets Authority opened a probe on October 30, and a São Paulo court issued an order on November 3. Nieman Lab reported that two US senators, Adam Schiff and Marsha Blackburn, wrote to Mark Zuckerberg asking him to withdraw the terms.

The November 18 post names regulators only in phase 3. Meta does not mention the court orders or the CMA probe.

## What publishers and advertisers should do now

![Steps for publishers and advertisers during the Meta commercial terms consultation](/images/infographics/ig-meta-commercial-terms-consultation-2027-2.webp)

These are Brandella's recommendations, not Meta guidance.

1. Keep working under the current terms. Nothing new needs accepting today, and Meta says it will give notice before any change.
2. Watch for the notice. Make sure the email on your Meta Business account reaches someone who reads legal and policy updates, so the review period is not missed.
3. Check your robots.txt now. Meta points to its crawler documentation, so decide which Meta user agents you allow. Our [robots.txt guide](/robots-txt-complete-guide/) covers the syntax, and our earlier post lists the five Meta crawlers.
4. Look at your server logs. See which Meta bots already visit and how often, the same way you would for other [AI bot traffic](/ai-bot-traffic-is-exploding/).
5. If you are a publisher, take part. Meta says it is briefing news publishers and industry groups. If your trade body is involved, give it your position before the early 2027 round.
6. Read the explainer when it lands. Meta says one will come with the updated terms. Compare it with the October preview before you accept anything.

## FAQ about the Meta commercial terms consultation

### When will the new Meta commercial terms take effect?

Meta has not given a date. It says the phased approach may take a few months, with further engagement in early 2027, and that account holders will get notice before new terms apply.

### Which terms are paused?

The Meta Commercial Terms, Product Catalog Terms, Self-Serve Ad Terms and Ad Creative Generative AI Terms, according to Meta's November 5 notice as reported by Nieman Lab.

### Who is Meta consulting?

News publishers across markets and global industry groups now, then news publishers, other stakeholders and regulators in early 2027.

### Does Meta respect robots.txt?

Meta's November 18 post says it follows the industry standard robots.txt mechanism as described in its web crawler developer documentation.
