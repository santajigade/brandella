Google Business Profile appeals are quicker to file now. Google has replaced the old multi step appeal workflow with a single form, as [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-business-profiles-appeals-form-simplified-42593.html) reported after Ben Fisher of Steady Demand spotted the change and posted about it on LinkedIn.

"It is one form now, not a whole funnel," Fisher wrote, adding that "You also get your case ID in less than 30 seconds!" Google has not published a statement about the change.

For local businesses, a suspended profile means lost calls, directions and reviews on Google Maps and Search. A shorter form helps, but the rules on Google's help pages did not get any looser. You still get one appeal, so it has to be right the first time.

![Google Business Profile appeals at a glance: one form, 1 appeal per decision, up to 5 business days for a decision, evidence optional](/images/infographics/ig-google-business-profile-appeals-form-1.webp)

## What changed in Google Business Profile appeals

Google had already added evidence uploads to the appeal workflow. Now the steps sit in one form instead of a funnel of separate screens. According to Fisher, the case ID now shows up within seconds of submitting.

The appeal steps on Google's [suspended profiles help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en) are still the same:

1. Open the [appeals tool](https://support.google.com/business/?p=manage_appeals) and confirm you are signed in to the right account.
2. Select the Business Profile, then the decision you want to appeal. The tool shows the decision date, the reason, a link to the policy, whether it can be appealed and its status.
3. Add optional evidence files and a short explanation.
4. Tick the box confirming you are ready and that your evidence is attached, then select "Submit appeal."

Google then reviews the appeal and emails you the decision.

## What you can appeal and where

Google's [appeals help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551?hl=en) splits the options by region.

| Restriction | UK and EEA businesses | Rest of world |
|---|---|---|
| Suspended or disabled profile | Appeals tool | Appeals tool |
| Rejected edits (name, category, address, phone, website) | Appeals tool | Contact Google support |
| Restricted media (post, photo, video) | Appeals tool | Contact Google support |
| Other content restrictions | Contact Google support | Contact Google support |
| Review removal decisions | Reviews management tool | Reviews management tool |

Two rules catch people out. A suspended or disabled profile "must be reinstated before you can appeal any content decisions." And if the Google Account itself is restricted, you first appeal that at the account restrictions page, then appeal the profile suspension separately.

## Evidence, time limits and second chances

Evidence is optional, but it is the strongest part of most appeals. Google lists official business registration, a business license, tax certificates and utility bills as examples. Its suspended profiles page says to "check that the business name and address match the profile" for every document you submit.

Google says "Appeals can only be submitted once" and that reviews and decisions "can take up to 5 business days." It also asks you not to send multiple appeals for the same issue before you get a decision. If your reinstatement request is denied, you can ask for an additional review through the [local appeals form](https://support.google.com/business/contact/local_appeals), with evidence you did not include the first time.

Agencies with many locations get a bulk path. When you appeal, the tool asks whether you are requesting bulk reinstatement for 10 or more suspended locations, and you can attach a spreadsheet of Business Profile IDs with your documents.

## How to file Google Business Profile appeals that succeed

![How to file a Business Profile appeal: read the policy reason, fix the issue first, gather matching documents, write a short explanation, submit once and wait](/images/infographics/ig-google-business-profile-appeals-form-2.webp)

These steps are our recommendations built on Google's rules:

1. Read the policy reason first. The tool links the policy behind the decision. Fix what it points to, whether it concerns the business name, address, category or something else, before you appeal.
2. Gather documents that match exactly. The business name and address on your registration, license or utility bill should match the profile character for character. Our guide to [NAP consistency](/nap-consistency-hidden-local-seo-ranking-factor/) explains why this matters.
3. Write a short, factual explanation. Say what the business is, where it serves customers and what you changed.
4. Submit once and wait. You get one appeal, and Google asks for up to 5 business days. A second appeal for the same issue will not speed things up.
5. Do not create a new profile. Google says not to make a new Business Profile for the same business.
6. Note the case ID. Save it with your evidence in case you need the additional review route.

Once you are back, protect the profile. Keep reviews within the rules as shown in our post on [how to get more Google reviews](/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/), and review your basics with our [Google Business Profile guide for beginners](/google-my-business-profile-complete-guide-beginners/).

## FAQ about Google Business Profile appeals

### How long does a Google Business Profile appeal take?

Google says appeal reviews and decisions can take up to 5 business days.

### How many times can I appeal a suspension?

Google says appeals can only be submitted once. If the reinstatement request is denied, you can request an additional review with new evidence.

### Is evidence required for an appeal?

No, evidence is optional. Google suggests documents such as a business registration, license, tax certificate or utility bill with a name and address that match the profile.

### Can I appeal rejected edits outside Europe?

Outside the UK and EEA, the appeals tool only covers suspended or disabled profiles. For rejected edits and media restrictions, Google directs you to its support team.
