ChatGPT Wallet is OpenAI's new payment layer that lets its AI agents, called dots, complete purchases for a shopper. OpenAI announced it on November 13, 2026 in its [Introducing ChatGPT Wallet](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/) post, and the rollout started the same day for a limited group of ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States.

The launch matters for anyone who sells online. Until now, most AI shopping help stopped at a recommendation and a link. With a wallet attached, the agent can go further: pick the product, fill the cart and pay, with the shopper approving the final step.

It is still an early and narrow release. But it is the clearest signal yet that AI assistants are becoming buyers on your website, not just sources of referral traffic, and stores that are hard for agents to use may lose those orders.

![ChatGPT Wallet launch key facts: November 13 2026 launch, US Pro subscribers 18 plus, Visa Mastercard and American Express partners](/images/infographics/ig-chatgpt-wallet-ai-agent-purchases-1.webp)

## What ChatGPT Wallet does

According to [TechCrunch's launch report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/), shoppers add an eligible card to ChatGPT Wallet once and then ask their dot to buy something. They do not re-enter payment details for each order. The dot also passes the merchant what it needs to fulfil the order, such as the shipping address and contact details.

OpenAI built the wallet with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, as [9to5Mac reported](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/13/openai-launches-chatgpt-wallet-enabling-dot-agents-to-make-purchases/). OpenAI describes it as the "first wallet that lets agents pay without access to users' actual card details."

The wallet works inside dot conversations. The dots themselves are OpenAI's personal agents, introduced in a separate [dots announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-dots/).

## How ChatGPT Wallet keeps card details hidden

The security model is the part merchants should understand, because it changes what lands in your checkout. Before each purchase, the shopper reviews the items, the total and the selected card, then approves it with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint. Each approval creates a purchase record.

After approval, the payment network issues a token that stands in for the real card number. The charge goes to the shopper's chosen card, but neither the dot nor the merchant ever sees the actual card details. OpenAI says shoppers keep their card rewards and the card's protections for eligible disputes.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Setup | Shopper adds an eligible US card to ChatGPT Wallet once |
| Shopping | The dot finds products and builds the order |
| Review | Shopper checks items, total and card |
| Approval | Face ID, fingerprint or similar security check for every purchase |
| Payment | Network token is sent instead of the real card number |
| Fulfilment | Merchant receives shipping and contact details and ships as normal |

## Who can use it right now

Access is deliberately small. TechCrunch reports the first release covers a restricted number of US ChatGPT Pro subscribers who are 18 or older and hold a US-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. OpenAI says the limited launch lets it test and improve the feature, and work with businesses, banks and payment networks on compatibility and reliability.

OpenAI also plans more control later. TechCrunch says future versions should allow spending limits and rules by purchase category or time period, so a shopper could pre-approve some purchases that the agent then handles on its own. For now, every purchase needs a manual approval.

There is one clear limitation: the wallet will not work on websites that block agents. TechCrunch notes that some retailers do block AI agents and that Amazon is the largest to do so. We covered a similar move in our post on the [United Airlines AI agents ban](/united-airlines-ai-agents-ban/).

## Why this matters for ecommerce marketers

Agent shopping is still small. TechCrunch points to reports that DoorDash and Uber have seen very few orders placed by AI agents so far. So this is not a reason to rebuild your store this week.

What changes is the direction of travel. Payment has been the missing piece for agent checkout, and three major card networks are now part of it. If agents can pay securely, more retailers are likely to test whether agent orders convert, and the stores that are easy for agents to read and use will be first in line.

It also links up with OpenAI's other recent moves for businesses. Brands can now claim a profile and launch an agent through the [ChatGPT Business Hub](/businesses-in-chatgpt-business-hub-agents/). Put together, OpenAI is building both sides: a place for businesses to present themselves and a way for shoppers' agents to buy from them.

## What online stores should do now

![Steps for online stores to prepare for ChatGPT Wallet agent purchases](/images/infographics/ig-chatgpt-wallet-ai-agent-purchases-2.webp)

These are Brandella recommendations based on what OpenAI and the launch reports have published so far, not official OpenAI requirements.

1. **Decide your agent policy on purpose.** Check your bot management, firewall and CDN settings. If they block AI agents by default, the wallet cannot complete a purchase on your site. Some brands will choose to block, and that is valid, but it should be a decision, not an accident.
2. **Read OpenAI's commerce guidance.** TechCrunch reports OpenAI published a Commerce Partner Playbook at [developers.openai.com/commerce](https://developers.openai.com/commerce) for sites that want to support agent shopping. Share it with your developers and payments team.
3. **Talk to your payment provider.** Ask how network tokens from agent purchases appear in your system and whether your fraud rules might decline them. A legitimate agent order can look unusual to an older risk model.
4. **Make product data clear and complete.** Agents rely on what they can read. Accurate prices, stock, variants, shipping costs and return terms on the page reduce failed or abandoned agent orders. Our guide to an [AI friendly website setup](/ai-friendly-website-chatgpt-gemini-claude-perplexity/) covers the basics.
5. **Keep checkout simple.** Fewer forced steps, clear error messages and guest checkout help both humans and agents finish an order.
6. **Measure agent activity separately.** Tag or segment orders and sessions that come from agents so you can see whether they convert and how often they are returned. Tools such as [Microsoft Clarity's agentic analytics](/microsoft-clarity-agentic-analytics/) show what agents do on your pages.

## What is still unclear

OpenAI has not said when ChatGPT Wallet will open to more users, more countries or plans beyond Pro. It has not published conversion data, order volumes or a list of merchants where agent checkout already works well.

We also do not yet know how merchants will be able to identify wallet orders in their own reporting, or how disputes on agent purchases will be handled in practice beyond the card protections OpenAI mentions. Treat this launch as an early test you can prepare for calmly, and watch for OpenAI's next update before making large changes.
