The 2026 Edelman LinkedIn B2B thought leadership report finds that buyers now read thought leadership through AI. In the [2026 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2026-b2b-thought-leadership-report), 84% of B2B decision makers said they used AI to summarize thought leadership while evaluating vendors, and 82% said it is becoming more valuable as AI becomes a common source of business answers.

Edelman published the findings on November 9, 2026 in an [article by Annie Dunleavy](https://www.edelman.com/insights/thought-leadership-isnt-just-what-you-say-its-what-people-do-it), its US head of content strategy. The survey covers more than 1,100 business executives across seven countries.

The message for content teams is clear. Your best articles and reports are now being summarized, forwarded and compared, often without anyone visiting your site. Content that is original, specific and easy for both people and AI to understand will carry further than content written to fill a calendar.

![B2B thought leadership report 2026 key numbers: 84 percent use AI summaries, 82 percent say value is rising, 89 percent share privately, 59 percent dropped a vendor](/images/infographics/ig-b2b-thought-leadership-report-2026-ai-1.webp)

## How the B2B thought leadership report survey was run

According to [PPC Land's breakdown of the report](https://ppc.land/82-of-b2b-buyers-value-thought-leadership-more-as-ai-spreads-edelman-finds/), this is the eighth edition of the series. It surveyed 1,135 business executives in the US, Canada, the UK, Germany, India, Australia and Singapore between September 4 and 21, 2026.

The results are seven country averages with no country breakdowns. All answers are self reported, so they measure what buyers say influences them, not tracked deals or win rates. Both Edelman and LinkedIn also sell services connected to this topic, which is worth keeping in mind.

## AI is now part of how buyers read your content

The headline finding is about AI. PPC Land reports that 84% used AI to summarize thought leadership, 72% used it to learn about a market and 67% used it to compare vendors.

When an AI tool cited a company's content, 78% went on to research that company and 70% read or viewed the cited content. PPC Land notes the base for these figures is unclear, because the report does not say what share of respondents use AI at all.

There is a gap on the supply side too. Among companies that produce thought leadership, 84% emphasize distribution through professional social feeds and 74% focus on traditional search, but only 46% optimize for AI generated answers.

## Most sharing happens in private

Buyers pass good content around, just not where you can see it. Edelman says 89% shared thought leadership through private channels such as email and direct messages, compared with 67% through social media or other public channels.

The reasons are practical. 73% shared because the piece had useful data, research or evidence, and 57% because it was relevant to a decision already under way. That is why engagement numbers alone undercount impact, and the report itself suggests brand lift studies, qualitative feedback and social listening as better measures.

## Thought leadership can win or lose the shortlist

The report also links content to vendor choice. Edelman says 76% found thought leadership shared in their buying group helpful for judging whether a vendor understood their needs, and 52% said it helped their group choose a vendor.

On the other side, 59% of final decision makers said poor or missing thought leadership contributed to a vendor being dropped. And when asked what builds trust most, 56% chose showing a clear understanding of their challenges, against only 14% who chose product or service strength.

| What buyers said | Share |
|---|---|
| Understanding our challenges builds the most trust | 56% |
| Product or service strength builds the most trust | 14% |
| Shared content helped judge if a vendor understood us | 76% |
| Poor or missing thought leadership helped drop a vendor | 59% |

## What content teams should do now

![Steps for B2B content teams based on the 2026 thought leadership report](/images/infographics/ig-b2b-thought-leadership-report-2026-ai-2.webp)

These are Brandella recommendations based on the report's findings.

1. **Lead with original data.** Useful research was the top reason to share at 73%. Even a small survey of your customers or an analysis of your own product data gives AI tools and buyers something new to cite.
2. **Write about the buyer's problem first.** Trust comes from showing you understand their challenges, not from feature lists. Open each piece with a specific problem your audience faces.
3. **Make every piece easy to summarize.** Clear headings, a short answer near the top, labelled numbers and named sources help AI tools summarize you accurately. Our [AI search optimization checklist](/ai-search-optimization-checklist-25/) covers the structure.
4. **Optimize for AI answers, not only feeds and search.** Only 46% of suppliers do this. Check how ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity describe your company and your topic, and fill the gaps, as explained in [how to increase AI search citations](/how-to-increase-ai-search-citations/).
5. **Design for private forwarding.** Offer a one page summary, a PDF or a clean shareable link that works well in email and chat, since that is where most sharing happens.
6. **Use credible outside voices.** PPC Land reports that customers and independent B2B creators scored highly for credibility. Partner with them, as covered in our guide to [B2B influencer marketing](/influencer-marketing-best-practices-b2b/).
7. **Measure beyond likes.** Add brand lift, sales conversations and buyer feedback to your content reporting.

## How this fits a wider content strategy

None of this replaces the basics of a solid plan. Thought leadership works best as one layer of a [complete content marketing strategy](/complete-content-marketing-strategy-guide-2026/), supported by evergreen guides and product content.

The shift is in who reads first. Increasingly it is an AI tool, then a buying group in private, and only then your website. Content that survives that chain with its key point intact is the content that keeps you on the shortlist.
