<p class="bx-lede">Backlinko's own website scores 62 on Semrush, 74 on Moz, and 90 on Ahrefs. Same domain, same backlink profile, three wildly different numbers. If you've ever obsessed over hitting a specific Domain Authority target, that gap is worth sitting with, because it reveals something most guides skip entirely: DA isn't a fixed score. It's one company's estimate, and it moves depending on who's estimating.</p>

<p>Domain Authority (DA) is a third-party metric developed by Moz, on a 1-100 scale, that predicts how likely a website is to rank well in search results based on the strength and diversity of its backlink profile.</p>

<p>It is not part of Google's actual ranking algorithm.</p>

<p>That distinction matters more than most SEO content admits. DA is a useful comparative tool, genuinely helpful for benchmarking against competitors, but treating it as a direct ranking lever leads to chasing the wrong metric entirely.</p>

<p>The natural, sustainable way to raise it comes down to the same fundamentals that improve real rankings anyway: earning links from a growing number of distinct, credible domains, keeping spam signals low, and building genuine topical depth.</p>

<div class="bx-answer">
<strong>The Direct Answer</strong>
<p>You increase Domain Authority naturally by earning backlinks from more unique, reputable, topically relevant domains, not by chasing DA itself. The score follows genuine link-building and content quality; it doesn't respond to being targeted directly.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="01the-same-site-three-different-scores"><span class="bx-num">01</span>The Same Site, Three Different Scores</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://backlinko.com/increase-domain-authority" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Backlinko's 7 proven ways to increase Domain Authority in 2026</a>, this discrepancy exists because each tool crawls the web independently and weighs signals differently. None is more "correct" than the others.</p>

<p>According to <a href="https://jeenaminfotech.com/blog/how-to-increase-domain-authority-scores/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jeenam Infotech's guide to 7+ ways to increase domain authority in 2026</a>, DA remains an important signal about the quality and influence of a website, even without being a direct Google ranking factor itself.</p>

<h2 id="02the-single-heaviest-input-linking-root-domains"><span class="bx-num">02</span>The Single Heaviest Input: Linking Root Domains</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://www.joinindexed.com/blog/how-to-improve-moz-domain-authority" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Indexed's guide to improving Moz Domain Authority</a>, ten links from ten different reputable sites are worth far more than fifty links from a single domain. Widening the count of distinct, credible domains matters more than raw volume.</p>

<div class="bx-checklist">
<div class="bx-check-item"><div class="bx-check-icon"></div><p><strong>Prioritize new referring domains</strong> over additional links from sites that already link to you.</p></div>
<div class="bx-check-item"><div class="bx-check-icon"></div><p><strong>Diversify link sources</strong> across guest posts, digital PR, and resource pages rather than one channel alone.</p></div>
<div class="bx-check-item"><div class="bx-check-icon"></div><p><strong>Favor topical relevance</strong> over raw authority when choosing where to pursue a link.</p></div>
</div>

<h2 id="03the-spam-score-myth"><span class="bx-num">03</span>The Spam Score Myth</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://growresolve.com/grow-your-domain-authority/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Resolve's guide to what Domain Authority is and how to improve it</a>, a stray low-quality link, or one from a site that has since gone downhill, will not get you penalized.</p>

<p>Modern Google's SpamBrain system simply nullifies unnatural links rather than punishing your site for them.</p>

<p>The links that actually cause trouble are ones deliberately placed where your brand doesn't belong, not the occasional weak link that appears naturally over time.</p>

<h2 id="05why-fast-artificial-growth-backfires"><span class="bx-num">05</span>Why Fast, Artificial Growth Backfires</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://smithdigital.io/blog/improve-domain-authority" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Smith Digital's guide to increasing Domain Authority in 2026</a>, buying backlinks or flooding low-quality directories may boost numbers briefly, but Google is quick to identify artificial patterns. Authority increasing too fast without legitimate signals can trigger a review, or a drop.</p>

<p>According to <a href="https://www.searchlogistics.com/learn/seo/link-building/increase-domain-authority/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Search Logistics' guide to increasing Domain Authority in 2026</a>, DA is relative, calculated by comparing your site's link profile against the broader web rather than measured on some fixed, absolute standard.</p>

<h2 id="06why-your-score-can-drop-even-when-you-ve-done-nothing-wrong"><span class="bx-num">06</span>Why Your Score Can Drop Even When You've Done Nothing Wrong</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://www.joinindexed.com/blog/how-to-improve-moz-domain-authority" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Indexed's guide referenced above</a>, Moz periodically recalibrates its model against the wider web. A score can dip simply because the sites you're benchmarked against grew faster, not because your own profile weakened at all.</p>

<p>According to <a href="https://www.vazoola.com/resources/how-to-increase-domain-authority" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Vazoola's essential guide to increasing Domain Authority for 2026</a>, on-page SEO, optimizing titles, internal links, and content depth, supports this indirectly, since Page Authority gains typically lift overall Domain Authority alongside it.</p>

<h2 id="08faqs-on-increasing-domain-authority"><span class="bx-num">08</span>FAQs on Increasing Domain Authority</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>

<div class="bx-faq" id="bxFaq">
  <button class="bx-q">Is Domain Authority a Google ranking factor?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>No. DA is a third-party metric created by Moz. Google doesn't use it in its algorithm, though sites with strong link profiles do tend to correlate with higher DA and better rankings.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">Why do different tools show different authority scores for the same site?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>Each tool, Moz, Ahrefs, Semrush, crawls the web independently and weighs signals differently. There's no single "correct" score; pick one tool and track its trend over time.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">What's the single biggest lever for increasing Domain Authority?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>Growing the number of unique, reputable linking root domains. Ten links from ten different credible sites outweigh fifty links from one domain.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">Will a few low-quality backlinks hurt my Domain Authority?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>Generally no. Modern Google nullifies the value of unnatural links rather than penalizing the site. Deliberately placed spam is the real risk, not occasional weak links.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">How long does it take to naturally increase Domain Authority?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>Months, not weeks, for sustainable growth. The scale is logarithmic, so each additional point becomes harder to earn as your existing score rises.</p></div>
</div>
