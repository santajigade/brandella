PMax household income exclusions have arrived in Google Ads, at least for some accounts. Google's [Performance Max setup help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15864837) now says that "Household income exclusions are currently available to select advertisers in beta", alongside the age and gender exclusions that were already there.

[PPC News Feed spotted the help page change on November 12, 2026](https://ppcnewsfeed.com/ppc-news/2026-11/performance-max-adds-household-income-exclusions/), and PPC Land followed up on November 15. Google has not published a blog post or a timeline for wider availability.

For advertisers, this is one more brand safety style control in a campaign type that started with very few. It helps premium brands that keep paying for clicks from people unlikely to buy, and lenders or service businesses with strict audience rules.

![PMax household income exclusions brackets and markets](/images/infographics/ig-pmax-household-income-exclusions-beta-1.webp)

## What Google's help page says about PMax household income exclusions

The exclusions sit under "Demographic exclusions" in campaign settings. According to Google, "Selecting a household income option allows you to exclude users within specific income ranges" from being served ads for your Performance Max campaign.

There is one odd detail. The same page still says "Currently, demographic exclusions in Performance Max are limited to age and gender-based exclusions." So the page contradicts itself, which usually means documentation is catching up with a limited test. Do not assume your account has the option until you see it.

That page lists 20 markets for household income in Performance Max: Australia, Brazil, Canada, Colombia, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Israel, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, the Philippines, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, the United Arab Emirates, the United States and Vietnam. India being on the list makes this relevant for many of our readers.

## The Unknown bracket matters most

Google's advice is direct: "If this is a strict or regulatory requirement, ensure you exclude users of 'Unknown' household income ranges." Excluding a bracket only stops people Google has placed in that bracket. Everyone Google cannot classify still sees your ads unless you also exclude Unknown.

The trade off is reach. Unknown can be a large group, so excluding it may shrink volume sharply. Only do it when the rule is truly strict. Also check Google's policy on [restricted targeting in personalized advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465) before you use income in a sensitive vertical, so your exclusions and your ad policy obligations line up.

## What advertisers should do now

![Steps to test PMax household income exclusions](/images/infographics/ig-pmax-household-income-exclusions-beta-2.webp)

These steps are Brandella's recommendations, not Google guidance.

1. **Check your campaign settings.** Open a Performance Max campaign, go to Demographic exclusions and see whether household income appears.
2. **Review income data first.** In Search or Video campaigns, look at household income performance reports to see which ranges actually convert before excluding anything in PMax.
3. **Exclude narrowly.** Start with one clearly unprofitable range rather than several. Large exclusions can starve the algorithm of data.
4. **Decide on Unknown deliberately.** Exclude it only for strict or regulatory needs, as Google advises.
5. **Run a clean comparison.** Note the change date and compare conversions, cost per conversion and conversion value for two to four weeks.
6. **Keep audience signals aligned.** If you exclude lower income ranges, make sure your [audience signals](/google-ads-audience-signals-explained/) do not push the campaign toward the same people.
7. **Document it for the client.** Income exclusions are a business decision, so get sign off and record why each range was excluded.

## How this fits the bigger PMax picture

Performance Max has slowly gained controls advertisers asked for, from age and gender exclusions to better reporting, as we covered when [Google expanded Performance Max reporting](/google-ads-performance-max-reporting/). Household income is the next step. If you are still deciding between campaign types, our comparison of [Search campaigns vs Performance Max](/search-campaign-vs-performance-max/) and our [step by step PMax optimisation guide](/how-to-optimize-performance-max-campaign-step-by-step/) will help you choose where this control fits.

Be careful not to over correct. Income is an estimate, not a fact about each person, and excluding too much can hide valuable buyers. Use it as a trim, not a filter for everything. And remember that a beta feature can change before wider release, so revisit your settings whenever Google updates the help page.

## FAQ about PMax household income exclusions

### Can every advertiser use household income exclusions in PMax?

No. Google's help page says they are currently available to select advertisers in beta.

### Which household income ranges can I exclude?

Top 10%, 11-20%, 21-30%, 31-40%, 41-50%, Lower 50% and Unknown, according to Google's demographic targeting page.

### Is India one of the supported markets?

Yes. Google's demographic targeting page lists India among the 20 markets for household income in Performance Max.

### Should I exclude the Unknown household income group?

Google advises excluding Unknown only when the requirement is strict or regulatory. Otherwise it can cut a lot of reach.
