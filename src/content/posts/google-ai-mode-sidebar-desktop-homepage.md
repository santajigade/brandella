Google has started rolling out a Google AI Mode sidebar on the desktop homepage. Robby Stein, VP of Product for Search at Google, [announced it on X](https://x.com/rmstein/status/2123500235930640844) on November 19, 2026: "New on the Google desktop homepage globally: we're rolling out a collapsible sidebar to easily find & jump back into your recent AI searches."

It affects anyone who opens google.com on a computer. Past AI Mode conversations now sit on the left of the homepage, ready to reopen.

For marketers, this points to longer AI Mode sessions. Stein's post links the feature to people coming to Search "with more questions that aren't just one and done," as quoted by [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-rolls-out-ai-mode-sidebar-42580.html).

![Google AI Mode sidebar: what the new desktop panel includes](/images/infographics/ig-google-ai-mode-sidebar-desktop-homepage-1.webp)

## What the Google AI Mode sidebar shows

[Digital Trends describes the layout](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/google-search-is-starting-to-look-a-lot-like-a-chatbot-app/) in the most detail. A thin rail runs down the left edge of the homepage with the Google "G" icon for a new thread and a settings gear.

Clicking the rail opens a panel with:

- New thread, to start a fresh AI Mode conversation
- Search threads, to find an older conversation
- Notes, with an "Add notebook" option
- Recent, a list of past AI Mode conversations

Clicking a past thread opens it in AI Mode with a box at the bottom to continue the conversation. The panel can be collapsed again to get the plain homepage back.

Search Engine Roundtable describes it as "a much simpler way to pick up where you left off." Its editor, Barry Schwartz, adds that he personally does not like the interface but thinks it may lead to more queries.

## How the rollout works

Stein's post says "globally," and Digital Trends also reports the rollout as worldwide on desktop. Neither source names specific countries or languages, and neither mentions mobile.

The rollout is gradual. Digital Trends says it "may take days or even weeks to appear for everyone." Neither source says whether you must be signed in to see your history.

Google has tested this before. On October 14, 2026, [Search Engine Roundtable reported a left sidebar](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-mode-left-sidebar-42298.html) from AI Mode showing on regular Google results, spotted by Damien Andell. At the time, Schwartz could not reproduce it and suspected a test.

## More Google features that point to AI Mode

The sidebar is one of several changes seen the same week that send people from classic search into AI Mode. All of these were spotted by users, not announced by Google:

| Feature | What was seen | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collapsible sidebar on the homepage | Recent AI searches on the left of google.com | Announced, rolling out globally |
| Lens button in Google Images | Opens an AI Mode chat with the image in the Google app | Spotted November 19 |
| "Show original results" button | Appears when a query has random typo characters | Spotted in testing |

On the Lens change, [Search Engine Roundtable notes](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-images-lens-to-ai-mode-42570.html) that outside the Google app the same button led to AI Overviews instead. Schwartz's comment was: "It is interesting to see Google keep funneling everything into AI Mode."

## What the AI Mode sidebar means for SEO and content

When people can return to a thread, one question turns into a running conversation. Your page may be cited in the first answer and missing from the fifth, or the other way round, so it helps to check citations across a whole thread.

It may also mean fewer fresh searches from the homepage for some topics, because people continue an old thread instead. That is our reading, not a Google statement, and there is no data on it yet.

## What marketers should do now

![Steps for marketers after the Google AI Mode sidebar rollout](/images/infographics/ig-google-ai-mode-sidebar-desktop-homepage-2.webp)

These steps are Brandella recommendations, not Google guidance.

1. Open google.com on desktop and look for the sidebar yourself. Run a few AI Mode threads on your main topics and note which sites get cited at each step.
2. Map the follow up questions people ask after the first one. Cover them on the same page or in clearly linked pages, so you stay useful deeper into the thread.
3. Read how [Google AI Mode decides which websites to cite](/how-google-ai-mode-decides-cite-websites/) and check your key pages against it.
4. Separate AI Mode visits in analytics where you can. Google's guide on [identifying Google Search traffic](/identify-google-search-traffic-referer/) explains the referer details.
5. Mark November 19, 2026 in your reports, so you can compare desktop Google traffic before and after the rollout.
6. Share the change with your team. If they are new to the feature, start with our explainer on [what Google AI Mode is](/what-is-google-ai-mode/).

## FAQ about the Google AI Mode sidebar

### Is the Google AI Mode sidebar on mobile?

Stein's post refers to the desktop homepage. Neither source we read mentions mobile.

### Can I hide the sidebar?

Yes, it is collapsible. Digital Trends says you can collapse the panel to get back to the plain homepage.

### Does the sidebar show Gemini app chats?

Google says it shows your recent AI searches. When an earlier version was tested in October, Marie Haynes said it showed conversations from the Gemini overlay on Android, but Google has not confirmed that for the new sidebar.

### Why do I not see it yet?

The rollout is gradual. Digital Trends says it may take days or weeks to reach everyone.
