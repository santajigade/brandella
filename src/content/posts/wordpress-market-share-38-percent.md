WordPress market share has dropped to 38.4% of all websites, according to the [W3Techs WordPress usage page](https://w3techs.com/technologies/details/cm-wordpress) dated November 25, 2026. Among sites where W3Techs can identify the content management system, WordPress still has 57.3%.

The fall is steep by WordPress standards. [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/wordpress-drops-to-38-5-of-the-web-down-nearly-5-points-in-a-year/598175/), working from W3Techs history data, reports that WordPress was on 43.2% of all websites a year earlier, a drop of 4.8 points. Over the same period, sites with no detected CMS grew from 28.6% to 33.0%.

If you build, run or market WordPress sites, nothing breaks today, and WordPress is still far ahead of every rival. The numbers do show where new websites are going, which affects the platforms your SEO, tracking and development skills need to cover.

## What the W3Techs numbers say

W3Techs reports two figures for each platform. Usage is the share of all websites. Market share is the share of websites that use a CMS it can detect. Its [CMS overview](https://w3techs.com/technologies/overview/content_management) says "33% of the websites use none of the content management systems that we monitor."

## How fast WordPress market share is falling

Search Engine Journal's month by month reading of W3Techs data shows the decline speeding up. WordPress fell by less than 0.5 points in each of the first seven months of the year, then 0.5 points in August, 0.5 in September and 0.7 in October. In November alone it had already lost 1.1 points by November 23.

On the market share measure, the same report says WordPress went from 60.5% to 57.3% of CMS sites over the year. Shopify rose from 4.9% to 5.5% of all websites and Wix from 4.1% to 4.2%. No single rival is taking WordPress's place. The bigger change is the growth of sites W3Techs cannot match to any CMS it tracks.

## Why more sites show no detected CMS

W3Techs does not explain the cause on its pages, and neither source proves one. Search Engine Journal points to sites built with AI tools, and notes that W3Techs now lists Lovable, an AI website builder, at 0.5% of all websites with no data before November.

W3Techs measures what it can detect on the sites it surveys, so a custom built or AI generated site with no known CMS lands in the "none" group. A falling share also does not mean WordPress sites are being deleted. It means the rest of the web is growing faster.

## WordPress versions and plugins in use

Most owners keep up with releases, according to the W3Techs WordPress page. Version 7 runs on 71.0% of WordPress sites, version 6 on 22.6%, version 5 on 4.4% and version 4 on 1.9%. Among page builders and add ons, Elementor appears on 31.4% of WordPress sites and WooCommerce on 19.6%.

That still leaves almost three in ten WordPress sites on older major versions. If you manage any of them, our [WordPress 7.2 field guide](/wordpress-7-2-field-guide/) and the [WordPress 7.1.5 security release](/wordpress-7-1-5-security-release/) notes cover what to check before you update.

## What site owners and developers should do now

![What site owners should do now: do not panic migrate, update old WordPress versions, check SEO basics on non CMS sites, learn Shopify and Wix, track your own data](/images/infographics/ig-wordpress-market-share-38-percent-2.webp)

These are our recommendations based on the numbers above:

1. Do not migrate because of a headline. A 38.4% share is still about seven times Shopify's. Pick a platform on cost, skills and features, not on trend charts.
2. Update old WordPress installs. With 22.6% of WordPress sites on version 6 and smaller groups on 5 and 4, many sites are missing security fixes. Review plugins too, as we explained in our post on [Rank Math vulnerabilities in 2026](/rank-math-vulnerabilities-2026/).
3. Check SEO basics on sites built without a CMS. AI built and custom sites often skip things a CMS adds for you, such as titles, canonical tags, XML sitemaps and structured data. Run a crawl before launch.
4. Learn the other platforms. Shopify, Wix and Squarespace together cover 12.1% of all websites. Agencies and freelancers will get more of these projects.
5. Measure Core Web Vitals on every stack. Speed problems look different on each platform. Our guide on [how to measure Core Web Vitals](/how-to-measure-core-web-vitals-tools-guide/) works for any of them.
6. Track the trend yourself. W3Techs says its reports are updated daily, so check the WordPress page monthly instead of relying on one snapshot.
