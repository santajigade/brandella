UK news publishers Google visibility dropped for most of the country's biggest news brands over the past year. According to [Press Gazette's analysis of Sistrix data](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media-audience-and-business-data/most-major-uk-news-publishers-saw-google-search-visibility-fall-in-2026/), published on November 10, 2026, more than two thirds of the largest UK news websites saw their organic visibility fall between October 2025 and October 2026.

The losers include household names such as BBC News and the Daily Mail. A few sites went the other way, most notably Substack, which roughly doubled its score. The shift matters well beyond news, because it shows how a domain change and a broader push for variety in results can reshape who wins in Google.

Below we break down the numbers, explain what the Sistrix score actually measures, and give a practical checklist any site owner can use to spot the same patterns early.

![UK news publishers Google visibility in Sistrix: BBC News 41.8 to 20.35, NYT 58.09 to 38.23, Substack 13.48 to 27.94](/images/infographics/ig-uk-news-publishers-google-visibility-2026-1.webp)

## UK news publishers Google visibility: what Sistrix shows

Press Gazette compared Sistrix Visibility Index scores from October 21, 2026 with the same point a year earlier (Substack's starting point is October 20, 2025). Here are the figures the article reported.

| Site | A year earlier | October 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Daily Mail | 32.17 (dailymail.co.uk) | 9.99 (dailymail.com) |
| BBC News | 41.8 | 20.35 |
| The New York Times | 58.09 | 38.23 |
| The Guardian | 236.88 | 216.86 |
| Substack | 13.48 | 27.94 |
| Money Saving Expert | 81.82 | 94.14 |
| The Times | 12.58 | 17.78 |

BBC News lost more than half its score. The Guardian dropped too, but it still has by far the highest score in the table. Money Saving Expert and The Times are among the brands that grew.

## What the Sistrix Visibility Index measures

The Visibility Index is not a traffic number. Sistrix explains in its [Visibility Index guide](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/sistrix-visibility-index/) that it tracks a large keyword set, records the top 100 results for each keyword, and weights each ranking by expected click rate for the position and by search volume. The sum becomes the score.

Press Gazette adds that the index only counts organic results. It excludes Top Stories boxes, AI Overviews and the Discover feed, which are major traffic sources for news brands. So a falling score means fewer and lower organic rankings across the tracked keywords, not necessarily the same percentage loss in visits.

Our view: treat any third party visibility score as a trend line. Confirm it against your own [Search Console Performance report](/search-console-performance-report/) before you change strategy.

## Why the Daily Mail drop is a domain migration story

The Daily Mail figure compares two different domains: dailymail.co.uk a year ago and dailymail.com now. SEO consultant Barry Adams of Polemic Digital told Press Gazette that "A domain name change is the most disruptive migration for any news publisher."

Google's own [site move guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) is clear that some disruption is expected. It says to "Expect temporary fluctuation in site ranking during the move," recommends server side permanent redirects, and says to keep redirects "generally at least 1 year." It also asks site owners to submit Change of Address requests for all verified variants of the old domain.

If you are planning any domain change, our guide on [301 vs 302 redirects](/301-vs-302-redirects-google-ranking-signals/) explains which redirect type to use and why.

## Big brands down, smaller sites up

For the drops at large news brands, Adams told Press Gazette they are "likely due to Google's focus on greater diversity in its results." He added: "Since search is now a zero-sum game, small sites winning means big sites lose."

On Substack's rise, Adams said: "A key advantage Substack has is that it's a self-curated platform where unique, quality content from authentic creators gains more traction." Those are his views, not a Google statement. Still, the table supports the general idea: brands with a clear specialist focus, such as Money Saving Expert, grew while larger general news brands slipped.

This fits with what other publishers are saying. Ziff Davis recently told investors its Google referrals fell about 37% in Q3, which we covered in [Ziff Davis Google referrals fell 37%](/ziff-davis-google-referrals-down-37-percent/).

## How to check if your site is losing visibility the same way

You do not need to be a national newspaper to use these lessons. This is Brandella's recommended checklist, not official Google guidance.

![Six step checklist to check whether your site is losing Google visibility, from Search Console trends to redirect audits](/images/infographics/ig-uk-news-publishers-google-visibility-2026-2.webp)

1. **Compare 12 months, not 4 weeks.** In Search Console, compare clicks and impressions for the last 3 months against the same 3 months a year ago. Slow declines hide in short date ranges.
2. **Split by page type.** Filter by folders such as /news/, /guides/ or /product/. A drop that sits in one section usually has a specific cause.
3. **Check which queries you lost.** Sort queries by click change. If you lost broad, generic queries but kept branded ones, you may be competing against more varied results, not suffering a technical fault.
4. **Audit redirects after any migration.** Crawl your old URLs and confirm every one returns a single 301 to the right new page, with no chains or loops.
5. **Look at who replaced you.** Search your top lost queries and note which sites now rank. If smaller specialist sites took your spot, study what first hand detail they offer that your page does not.
6. **Strengthen experience signals.** Add author bylines with real credentials, original data and first hand testing, as explained in our guide to [EEAT](/eeat-experience-expertise-authority-trust/).

## What site owners should take from this

The headline is not that news SEO is broken. It is that scale alone no longer guarantees rankings. Brands with distinctive, expert content, such as Money Saving Expert, grew while larger general brands slipped.

Two practical rules stand out. First, a domain change carries real risk, so plan it with Google's site move steps and give it time. Second, if bigger competitors are losing ground to smaller voices, that is a chance for focused sites to win with depth and genuine expertise.

Keep watching your own data monthly. If your visibility falls on generic topics while niche, expert pages hold up, that is the same shift Sistrix is picking up at national scale.
