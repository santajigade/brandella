How Americans use ChatGPT is now clearer thanks to a new study from SparkToro. Co-founder Rand Fishkin published [the analysis](https://sparktoro.com/blog/we-analyzed-11800-chatgpt-prompts-from-12000-users-to-answer-how-do-americans-use-ai-tools-in-2026/) on November 17, 2026. It covers 41,961 complete ChatGPT conversations, 118,163 prompts and 12,321 US users between January 1 and October 29, 2026.

The headline finding for marketers is simple. Most ChatGPT use is personal and consumer focused, and only a small share involves researching what to buy. In Fishkin's words: "ChatGPT is a heavily-consumer app, and anyone who tells you otherwise is wrong."

That does not mean AI visibility is a waste of time. It means brands should be realistic about where buying research happens and plan AI work alongside search, not instead of it.

![Infographic with key numbers from the SparkToro study on how Americans use ChatGPT](/images/infographics/ig-how-americans-use-chatgpt-sparktoro-study-1.webp)

## How the SparkToro ChatGPT study worked

The data came from [Verso](https://tryverso.ai/breakdown), a company that supplies anonymised, consent based chatbot conversation data. SparkToro's panel connected to OpenAI accounts on an opt in basis.

A few method details matter when you read the numbers:

- 131 heavy users (more than 33 prompts a month) were removed. They accounted for 21% of all prompts.
- 9,675 panelists with demographic records were weighted to 2024 US Census targets. No category moved by more than 1.4 points.
- Claude labelled each conversation using a codebook of 34 intents grouped into 12 categories. A second labeller agreed on 90% of category labels.

## What Americans use ChatGPT for

Images, not search style questions, came out on top. Here is the share of conversations in each of SparkToro's 12 categories:

| Category | Share of conversations |
|---|---|
| Images and visual design | 16.1% |
| Information and entertainment | 14.0% |
| Everyday life and admin | 12.2% |
| Writing and communication | 11.9% |
| Health and wellness | 10.3% |
| Money, work and business | 8.5% |
| Schoolwork and learning | 7.6% |
| Shopping, brands and local services | 5.7% |
| Technology support and coding | 4.4% |
| Relationships and emotional support | 3.4% |
| Chit-chat and other | 3.0% |
| Creative writing and roleplay | 2.9% |

The image result fits with what OpenAI is doing commercially. ChatGPT now shows [visual ads during image generation](/chatgpt-visual-ads-image-generation/), which makes more sense when image work is the single biggest use.

## How often ChatGPT users research brands

SparkToro reports that only 6.9% of prompts "showed anything related to researching a potential brand to buy from." At the same time, 12.1% of responses contained one or more brands, products or companies, slightly more often than users asked for them.

Fishkin compares this with SparkToro's own [2024 study of about 332 million Google searches](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-we-analyzed-332-million-queries-over-21-months-to-uncover-never-before-published-data-on-how-people-use-google/). In that data, 14.5% of searches had commercial intent and 0.69% had transactional intent. His conservative guess is that people ask Google purchase journey queries at "16X+ the rate of ChatGPT."

That 16X figure is his estimate, and the two studies use different methods. Still, the direction is clear: Google remains the bigger stage for buying research. Our comparison of [AI search vs traditional search](/ai-search-vs-traditional-search/) explains why both still need attention.

## Limits of the data

SparkToro is open about the gaps, and you should be too before you quote these numbers in a strategy deck.

- **No API usage.** The panel does not include requests made through the OpenAI API. Fishkin guesses API traffic is 50% to 75% of total usage, but that is his estimate.
- **Panel skew.** Panelists skew younger, female, lower income and mostly mobile. Desktop and work account use is likely under counted.
- **What, not how much.** Fishkin says the panel "tells us *what* people ask ChatGPT far better than *how much* they use it."

## What marketers should do now

![Checklist for marketers after the SparkToro study on how Americans use ChatGPT](/images/infographics/ig-how-americans-use-chatgpt-sparktoro-study-2.webp)

These steps are Brandella's recommendations based on the study, not instructions from SparkToro.

1. **Keep Google first for buying research.** If your goal is sales, rankings and AI Overviews visibility on Google still matter most.
2. **Measure AI visibility, but across engines.** Fishkin says ChatGPT tracking is useful "only if you're also tracking your keyword rankings and AI visibility on Bing." Our [Bing Webmaster Tools guide](/bing-webmaster-tools-complete-guide/) covers the basics.
3. **Map your category to the table.** A design, health or everyday life brand sits in a large ChatGPT use case. A B2B software brand may sit in a much smaller one.
4. **Earn mentions that AI answers can reuse.** Since 12.1% of answers named a brand, make sure your brand is described clearly on trusted sites. See [how ChatGPT chooses websites for its answers](/how-chatgpt-chooses-websites-for-answers/).
5. **Check ChatGPT referrals in analytics.** Compare them with your Google traffic before moving budget. Our earlier look at [ChatGPT external links in chats](/chatgpt-external-links-half-of-chats/) shows how clicks can happen.

## FAQ about how Americans use ChatGPT

### What is the most common use of ChatGPT in the US?

In SparkToro's panel, images and visual design was the largest category at 16.1% of conversations, followed by information and entertainment at 14.0%.

### How much ChatGPT use involves shopping?

Shopping, brands and local services made up 5.7% of conversations. Only 6.9% of prompts showed research into a brand to buy from.

### Does this mean AI search optimisation is not worth it?

No. Fishkin writes that "AI visibility matters a huge amount," but says the reason is not ChatGPT alone. Plan AI visibility together with Google and Bing.

### Who provided the data?

Verso supplied opt in, anonymised ChatGPT conversation data from a US panel. SparkToro did the analysis.
