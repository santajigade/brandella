The Google Ads sexual content policy page now spells out far more clearly what counts as restricted and what is allowed. Google logged the change in its [policy change log](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/18952957?hl=en) on November 16, 2026: "Google has updated the Sexual content policy article to provide additional examples and clearer information to advertisers. This does not change enforcement of the policy."

The rules have not changed. The examples just make the line easier to see. That helps lingerie brands, sexual wellness shops, pharmacies, fitness studios and publishers, who often get ads limited without knowing which detail triggered it. Google's change log also lists an earlier update to the same article in October 2026, so this is the second edit in about a month. [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-expands-sexual-content-advertising-policy-42548.html) estimated that the new version adds about 300 words.

## How the Google Ads sexual content policy is structured

The [Sexual content policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6023699) says Google "restricts certain kinds of sexual content in ads and destinations." Such ads show "in limited scenarios based on the user's preferences, search queries, and age, as well as local laws," and "Ads must not target minors."

There are two tiers. Nudity is the only "strongly restricted" category; those ads serve only on sexual-content search queries, and also depend on the user's age, local laws and SafeSearch settings. Partial nudity, sexual merchandise, sexual entertainment and sexually suggestive elements are "moderately restricted" and serve based on age, local laws and SafeSearch settings. Google calls its example lists guidance, "not complete lists."

## Restricted vs allowed: the new examples

The most useful part for advertisers is the side-by-side examples. Here is how Google now separates them in three of the categories.

| Category | Restricted examples | Allowed examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partial nudity | Lower or side curve of an exposed female breast; majority of buttocks exposed, such as a thong or g-string | Majority of buttocks covered, such as bikini or tanga cut underwear; partial breast nudity in an educational or breastfeeding context |
| Sexual merchandise | Sex toys; devices that aid sex, such as vacuum erection devices or sex position pillows; condoms promoted for sexual pleasure; fetish lingerie mainly for sexual experiences | Pelvic floor muscle trainers; hormone replacement therapy; non-sexual genital health products; condoms promoted for preventing pregnancy or STIs without suggestive marketing; corsets, garter belts and lace bras |
| Sexual entertainment | Strip clubs; lap dancing, pole dancing, burlesque and stripteases; adult film festivals; erotica promoted with sexualized language; phone sex and webcams; adult games with sexualized narratives; sexualized gifts and party favors | Romance games without passionate imagery; pole dancing classes for exercise; publications with sexual content promoted without sexualized language |

For nudity, simple sketches such as stick figures without intimate detail, and nudity in classical or historic art "which has a wide general audience for all ages", are allowed.

The policy also has detailed examples for sexually suggestive elements. Close-ups or slow zooms on breasts with no contextual reason are restricted, while breasts shown in a bra advertisement are allowed. Sexy dancing in private or while undressed is restricted, but sexy dancing in public or for exercise is allowed. Emojis that allude to sex acts are restricted; emojis unrelated to sex are fine.

## Where restricted ads can and cannot run

The restriction is not only about who sees the ad. The policy page says sexual content ads can run on Google Ads and the Search Network, but not on AdMob, Google Ad Manager, YouTube, the Display Network or the Google Ad Manager Network.

Some formats cannot carry this content at all: app ads and app assets, Dynamic Display Ads, Gmail ads, image ads, reservation display ads and TrueView video ads. Sexual content does not serve in 20 countries, including India, the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia. Strongly restricted content also does not serve in 14 more, including Germany, Singapore and South Korea.

For Indian advertisers that point matters most. If your campaign targets India, restricted sexual content will not serve there whatever the format.

## What advertisers should do now

These steps are Brandella's recommendations based on Google's policy page.

![What to do now about the Google Ads sexual content policy: five steps](/images/infographics/ig-google-ads-sexual-content-policy-examples-2.webp)

1. List every ad in your account that is limited or disapproved under the Sexual content policy.
2. Compare each one with the new examples. A lingerie ad showing a thong falls under partial nudity, while a lace bra product falls under the allowed lingerie example.
3. Rewrite copy for health and wellness products so it stays on the health benefit. Google allows condoms promoted for preventing pregnancy or STIs "without sexually suggestive marketing", and allows pelvic floor trainers.
4. Check targeting. Remove countries where the content cannot serve, and move restricted creatives out of YouTube, Display and Gmail campaigns.
5. After you edit an ad, Google re-reviews it, typically in 24 to 48 hours. Appeal through your account if you think an ad was wrongly limited.

Remember that Google gives a warning at least 7 days before suspending an account under this policy, so act on any warning at once. ## FAQ about the Google Ads sexual content policy

### Does this update change how Google enforces the policy?

No. Google's change log says the update adds examples and clearer information and "does not change enforcement of the policy."

### Can lingerie brands advertise on Google Ads?

Yes. Lingerie generally worn under clothing, such as corsets, garter belts and lace bras, is listed as allowed. Fetish lingerie mainly intended for sexual experiences is restricted.

### Can sexual content ads run on YouTube?

No. The policy page says sexual content ads cannot run on YouTube, AdMob or Google Ad Manager, or on the Display Network.

### Will sexual wellness ads show in India?

Restricted sexual content will not. India is on Google's list of countries where all sexual content will not serve, so check which of your products fall under the restricted examples before targeting India.
