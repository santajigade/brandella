Google published its November Demand Gen Drop on November 18, 2026, and the theme is the holiday shopping rush. The [official Ads and Commerce blog post](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/demand-gen-drop-november-2026/) groups four features that help advertisers "find and convert high-value shoppers on YouTube": product feeds, faster checkout, creator partnerships boost and seasonality adjustments for Smart Bidding.

This Demand Gen drop matters most to retailers and ecommerce brands that already run Demand Gen campaigns, or that plan to add YouTube to their Black Friday and Cyber Monday mix. None of the four features is brand new to Google Ads, but seasonality adjustments are described as "newly available in Demand Gen campaigns", and together they form a clear holiday checklist.

The timing is tight. Black Friday is days away, so the useful question is not what Google announced but which of these settings you can switch on this week without breaking a campaign that is already learning.

## What is in the November 2026 Demand Gen drop

The header image calls this "drop #15", and Google points readers to its [Demand Gen Drops hub](https://business.google.com/us/accelerate/demand-gen-drops/) for the full archive. Here is what the four features do and the numbers Google shared for each.

| Feature | What it does | Result Google cites |
|---|---|---|
| Product feeds on YouTube | Shows Merchant Center items inside Demand Gen ads | Petco: 6% lift in purchase intent, 71% surge in store visits, 30% boost in Omni ROAS |
| Faster checkout | Checkout links or Universal Commerce Protocol checkout cut steps to purchase | 11% average increase in conversion value at similar CPA or ROAS (US, from Google's product feeds guide) |
| Creator partnerships boost | Runs creator videos as ads | Javvy Coffee: 50% higher conversion rates; Shopee: 9x average ROI with affiliate partnerships boost |
| Seasonality adjustments | Tells Target CPA and Target ROAS to expect a conversion rate jump | No figure given |

All results are advertiser case studies published by Google, not independent tests. Treat them as a sign of what is possible, not as a forecast for your account.

## Product feeds and faster checkout

Product feeds connect a Merchant Center feed to a Demand Gen campaign so your catalog appears with your image and video creatives. Google's [product feeds guide (PDF)](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/demand_gen_product_feeds.pdf) says you turn it on in Campaign settings under "Product feeds" with the option "Run a product feed campaign", then choose the Merchant Center account and the feed label you want to advertise. Ads can then show across YouTube, Discover, Gmail and other Google placements.

The faster checkout part relies on [checkout links in Merchant Center](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13945960). You add a `checkout_link_template` attribute (or schema markup on crawled pages) so a ready shopper can jump straight to your cart or checkout page. Google notes that checkout links currently show only on mobile and tablet, not desktop, and that the checkout page must not require sign in. It should also show your brand, price and product details, because the shopper may skip the product page entirely.

Universal Commerce Protocol checkout is the second route Google mentions. It is linked from the post to a [Merchant Center help page](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/16837055); if you have not set it up yet, our view is that checkout links are the quicker option to add before the holidays.

## Seasonality adjustments arrive in Demand Gen

For most performance teams this is the most practical item in the Demand Gen drop. According to Google's [seasonality adjustments help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10369906), you can now use them in Demand Gen campaigns that bid with Target ROAS or Target CPA. You schedule an expected conversion rate change for a sale period, Smart Bidding bids more competitively from the first hour, and the campaign returns to normal when the event ends. Google says "no negative adjustment is needed when the promotion is over."

The limits are important. Google calls the feature "ideal for short events of 1 to 7 days" and warns it may work less well over long periods, such as more than 14 days. It also advises using it only when you expect a major conversion rate change, because Smart Bidding already accounts for normal seasonal patterns. A weekend flash sale fits. The whole of November does not.

If you manage many accounts, an adjustment created at manager account level can apply to all linked client accounts, though clients added later do not inherit it automatically.

## Creator partnerships boost for holiday reach

[Creator partnerships boost](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15223349) lets you run organic YouTube creator videos as ads. The creator starts the link to your Google Ads account, and linked videos appear in a "Received" tab. In Demand Gen, the co-branded partnership format can run in In-Feed and Shorts placements.

One detail that is easy to miss: Google says you are responsible for having the rights to use the video as an ad, which may need a separate agreement with the creator. Google also recommends a head to head experiment, with one campaign using your normal video and another adding the creator video, keeping budgets and targeting the same. If you are new to the campaign type, our [Demand Gen campaigns explained](/demand-gen-campaigns-explained/) guide covers the basics.

## What advertisers should do now

![What advertisers should do now with the November Demand Gen drop](/images/infographics/ig-demand-gen-drop-november-2026-2.webp)

These steps are Brandella's recommendations based on Google's documentation, ordered from fastest to slowest to set up.

1. **List your sale dates.** Write down each promotion with its exact start and end time. Only events of about 1 to 7 days are good candidates for a seasonality adjustment.
2. **Add seasonality adjustments to Target CPA or Target ROAS Demand Gen campaigns** for those short windows, and only where you expect a clear conversion rate jump.
3. **Check your Merchant Center feed** for errors and current prices before you connect it, especially after any recent issues like the [November 6 Merchant Center outage](/google-merchant-center-outage-november-6/).
4. **Turn on product feeds** in at least one Demand Gen campaign and pair the products with strong images and video.
5. **Test checkout links on a phone.** Open the checkout URL logged out and confirm the cart loads with the right product and price.
6. **Ask your creators to link their best videos** and confirm usage rights in writing before you boost them.
7. **Keep a control.** Change one thing per campaign so you can tell which feature moved results.

Also review your holiday creative. Google's earlier [Creative Drop 2 with holiday assets](/google-ads-creative-drop-2-holiday-ai-tools/) and the [Holiday 100 gift trends](/google-holiday-100-2026-trends-marketers/) are useful inputs for new images and video.

## FAQ about the Demand Gen drop

### What is a Demand Gen drop?
It is Google's recurring roundup of new and updated Demand Gen features. The November 2026 edition is labelled drop #15 and focuses on holiday shopping.

### Can I use seasonality adjustments with Maximize Conversions in Demand Gen?
Google's help page lists Demand Gen support only with Target ROAS or Target CPA bid strategies, so set a target first.

### Do checkout links work on desktop?
No. Google says checkout links currently appear only on mobile and tablet devices, in supported countries including the US.

### Are the Petco and Javvy Coffee results typical?
They are case studies chosen and published by Google. Your results will depend on your feed quality, creative and budget, so test against a control.
