Google released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, 2026. It follows the Carbon and Hydrogen launches earlier this month, and [Google's announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/) describes it as bringing "frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion" to software engineering, knowledge work and science.

Neon sits between the other two. Hydrogen is the fast model for everyday execution, Carbon is for the hardest long running work, and in Google's words "Neon balances depth and efficiency." It has four thinking levels and a 1 million token context window.

For marketers, the price is the main news. Neon costs $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, and Google claims it gets close to Carbon's quality for less money per task.

## Where Gemini 4 Neon is available

Google says Neon is rolling out now in these places:

- The Gemini API in Google AI Studio (paid tier) and in Android Studio. All AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can try it in AI Studio.
- Gemini for business, through the API and the model menu.
- Google Antigravity, for paid AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.
- The Gemini app, for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, who can pick it from the model menu.

[9to5Google reports](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/18/gemini-4-neon-announcement/) that the app's model picker now reads Auto, Everyday help (4 Hydrogen), Deeper analysis (4 Neon) and Code and complex work (4 Carbon). It also reports that Neon replaces Argon in the app and that the older 3.1 Pro model is going away.

The [Google Cloud developer guide](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/guides/gemini-4-neon) covers Neon on Google's enterprise agent platform. It lists a combined input and output limit of 1,048,576 tokens, with a default output of 65,536 tokens, and thinking levels LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH (the default) and XHIGH.

Google's announcement does not mention Neon in AI Mode or Google Search. We covered the search side separately for [Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Google Search](/gemini-4-hydrogen-google-search-seo/) and [Gemini 4 Carbon in AI Mode](/gemini-4-carbon-ai-mode-marketers/).

## Gemini 4 Neon pricing compared

Google lists Neon at $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 per million cached input tokens (which it calls a 95% discount) and $15 per million output tokens.

9to5Google puts that at twice the price of Hydrogen. It also reports that Argon and Carbon cost $2 per million input and $10 per million output until November 30, rising to $4 and $20 after that, which would make Neon 25% cheaper than those two from December.

| Model | Input per 1M tokens | Output per 1M tokens | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gemini 4 Neon | $3 | $15 | Google |
| Gemini 4 Neon, cached input | $0.15 | n/a | Google |
| Argon and Carbon until November 30 | $2 | $10 | 9to5Google |
| Argon and Carbon from December | $4 | $20 | 9to5Google |

Cost per task matters more than the list price. Google says Neon costs 32% less per task than Carbon at the high setting, and at its highest setting costs "36%+ less per task" than competing models at theirs.

## Benchmarks Google highlights

Google shares a long list of scores. A few are useful for marketing work:

- Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: 61 at the highest thinking level, third overall.
- Vals Index: second, at 70.9%.
- Zapier AutomationBench: first overall, at 56.3%.
- Chartography: 73.5%.
- Surge AI: first on most of its finance, accounting, spreadsheet and constraint following tests.

These are the scores Google chose to publish, so treat them as a starting point. Your own prompts and data are the test that counts.

## What marketers should do now

![Gemini 4 Neon: steps for marketing teams to test the model](/images/infographics/ig-gemini-4-neon-marketers-2.webp)

The steps below are Brandella recommendations, not Google guidance.

1. Pick three real jobs your team already gives to AI, such as a content brief, a competitor summary and a spreadsheet clean up. Run each on Hydrogen, Neon and Carbon with the same prompt and compare the output and the cost.
2. Try the thinking levels. A lower level may do for short copy, while long research or analysis may need high. Record which level each job needs.
3. If you send the same long instructions or brand guidelines with every request, keep them as a stable shared prefix so the cheaper cached input rate can apply.
4. If your tools call Argon or Carbon through the API, check your bills against the higher rates 9to5Google reports from December, and test whether Neon can take over some of those jobs.
5. On AI Pro or Ultra, choose Deeper analysis in the Gemini app for research and planning work, and keep Everyday help for quick drafts.
6. Keep a human edit on anything you publish. Benchmark scores say nothing about your brand voice or your facts.

## FAQ about Gemini 4 Neon

### Is Gemini 4 Neon free?

Not in the Gemini app. Google says Neon is for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers there. In the API it is billed at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens on the paid tier.

### How is Neon different from Hydrogen and Carbon?

Google places Neon in the middle. Hydrogen is for fast everyday tasks, Carbon is for the hardest long running work, and Neon aims for depth at a lower cost per task than Carbon.

### How big is the Gemini 4 Neon context window?

Google lists 1 million tokens. The Google Cloud guide gives the exact limit as 1,048,576 tokens, shared between input and output.

### Is Gemini 4 Neon used in Google Search?

Google's Neon announcement does not mention Search or AI Mode, so there is no confirmation that it powers search results.
