Walmart Meta Muse shopping went live on November 24, 2026. In its [announcement](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/11/black-friday-deals-with-meta-muse), Walmart says customers can now shop its Black Friday deals through Muse, Meta's personal AI agent, and check out inside the conversation.

It is the first time Walmart has opened its store to an outside agent through the Walmart Agent Gateway, which [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/walmart-teams-up-with-meta-to-bring-black-friday-deals-to-ai-agent-muse/) describes as a system that lets outside AI assistants connect directly to the retailer's online store. Walmart says Muse "is just the beginning" and that more apps and agents will follow.

If you sell through Walmart or plan for AI shopping, watch who decides what the shopper sees. Muse passes on the request, and Walmart picks the products.

![Walmart Meta Muse shopping at a glance: launched November 24, 2026, 3 steps to buy, Walmart picks the products, spending limits set by the shopper](/images/infographics/ig-walmart-meta-muse-agent-shopping-1.webp)

## How Walmart Meta Muse shopping works

Walmart lays the process out in three steps:

1. Tell Muse what you need. A shopper can ask for "a Black Friday TV deal under $500" or which Black Friday deals are available for delivery. Muse helps them explore products and build a basket.
2. Set your shopping rules. Muse sends the shopper to Walmart to sign in and then back to the chat. The first time an item goes in the cart, the shopper sets up an Agent Profile in their Walmart account, including a spending limit.
3. Check out. The purchase is completed in the conversation with a card saved in the Walmart account, at the same prices as Walmart.com. Walmart's return policy still applies.

Muse hands the shopping list to Walmart through the gateway, and Walmart "then does the shopping, finding products that match your request." TechCrunch puts it the same way: the assistant sends the request to Walmart, which returns relevant products and helps build the cart.

## Controls for shoppers and what Muse does not see

Walmart says Agent Profiles let shoppers set spending limits, approve purchases and check their purchase history to see which orders the agent made. TechCrunch adds that users can delete the agent's profile whenever they want.

On data, Walmart says the gateway helps agents find relevant products "without sharing customers' broader purchase history with others." TechCrunch goes a step further and reports that Muse does not get access to a customer's Walmart shopping history or other personal information.

## Walmart is opening up while Amazon pushes back

Walmart is welcoming outside agents at a time when, as TechCrunch reports, Amazon has taken steps to stop third party AI agents from buying on its site. TechCrunch also notes that Instacart recently joined forces with Muse and that Walmart plans to open the gateway to more developers and AI platforms, so Muse could end up as one of several assistants.

Walmart is not giving up its own front door either. Its own agent, Sparky, still sits on Walmart.com, and Walmart frames shopping now as four routes: in stores, online, with Sparky and through an agent.

## What it means for brands and Walmart sellers

Muse is the interface, but Walmart chooses which products come back for each request. If your product is not in Walmart's catalog with clear data, a good price and stock near the shopper, the agent has nothing to show.

The announcement says nothing about sponsored products in Muse or paid placement, and as [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/walmart-muse-agent-gateway/) points out, Walmart has not released transaction volume, conversion performance or an attribution method for the launch. Treat Muse as a new shopping interface for now, not a new ad channel. Anyone who tells you how to "rank in Muse" is guessing, because Walmart has not published how matching works.

## What marketers and Walmart sellers should do now

![What Walmart sellers should do now: check titles and attributes, price for Black Friday, keep stock and delivery accurate, test agent style requests, compare order data](/images/infographics/ig-walmart-meta-muse-agent-shopping-2.webp)

These steps are our recommendations, not Walmart guidance:

1. Audit your Walmart listings. Make sure titles, attributes, sizes and key specs answer the plain questions shoppers type, such as "TV under $500" or "toy for a 6 year old."
2. Get Black Friday pricing right. Muse checks out at Walmart.com prices, so a deal that is live on Walmart is the deal the agent sees.
3. Keep stock and delivery data accurate. Shoppers can ask for items available for delivery, and an item that is out of stock cannot be picked.
4. Test agent style requests yourself. Ask Muse for products in your category the way a shopper would and note which brands come back.
5. Watch your Walmart order data. Walmart has not shared attribution for agent orders, so compare this week with past Black Friday periods and note any change.
6. Plan for more agents. Walmart says more apps and agents are coming, so clean product data will pay off beyond Muse. Our guide to [Google Holiday 100 trends](/google-holiday-100-2026-trends-marketers/) can help you match holiday demand.

## FAQ about Walmart Meta Muse shopping

### When did Walmart Meta Muse shopping launch?

Walmart announced it on November 24, 2026 and said customers could shop Black Friday deals through Muse starting that day.

### Can Muse see my Walmart purchase history?

Walmart says the gateway works without sharing customers' broader purchase history with others. TechCrunch reports that Muse does not get access to Walmart shopping history or other personal information.

### Can brands advertise inside Muse?

Walmart has not announced any ad format or sponsored placement for Muse shopping.

### Can shoppers limit what the agent spends?

Yes. The Agent Profile in the Walmart account lets shoppers set a spending limit, approve purchases and review orders the agent placed.
