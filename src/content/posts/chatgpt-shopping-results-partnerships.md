OpenAI has changed how it describes ChatGPT shopping results. Its [Shopping with ChatGPT Search help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490) used to say product results were "not influenced by any OpenAI partnerships." That line is gone, as [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/chatgpt-product-results-may-be-influenced-by-openai-partnerships-42588.html) reported on November 23, 2026.

The page now says: "Product results are not ads; how products and merchants are selected is described below." OpenAI has not published a statement explaining the edit, so we do not know whether any partnership now affects results.

For online stores, the edit is a reason to look again at how ChatGPT picks products and sellers, and at the inputs you control.

![ChatGPT shopping results wording before and after: old line said not influenced by OpenAI partnerships, new line says not ads and points to selection details](/images/infographics/ig-chatgpt-shopping-results-partnerships-1.webp)

## What changed in the ChatGPT shopping results wording

Search Engine Roundtable compared the live page with an archived copy from early November.

| | Wording |
|---|---|
| Before | "Product results are selected independently by ChatGPT and are not ads, nor influenced by any OpenAI partnerships." |
| Now | "Product results are not ads; how products and merchants are selected is described below." |

Two claims dropped out: that results are selected "independently" and that partnerships play no part. The claim that product results are not ads stays, and the page still says ads are a separate thing with their own help article.

Removing a sentence is not the same as confirming a change in behaviour. Still, marketers who told clients that ChatGPT product picks are purely organic should stop saying it with the old certainty.

## How ChatGPT selects products now

The current page says ChatGPT may surface products when it judges them relevant to what the user wants. It uses the query plus context such as Memory and Custom instructions, and the page warns that the model can misread intent and that not every available product is shown.

OpenAI lists three inputs for choosing products:

- Structured metadata from first party and third party providers
- Model responses generated before new search results are considered
- OpenAI's safety standards and product policies

The page says the weight of each factor varies with the request. Price, reviews and features also come into play; the page's own example is a request for dog costumes.

## How merchants are ranked

When ChatGPT shows a product, it may also list merchants selling it. That list comes from merchant and product metadata supplied by third party providers or directly by merchants.

OpenAI says merchants are ranked on factors including availability, price, quality and whether the seller is the maker or primary seller. It also says it expects to personalise these rankings further. Prices come from third party providers, and the first merchant listed may not have the lowest price, although a "Best price" label can appear.

Labels such as "Budget-friendly" and review summaries are generated by the model and not verified by OpenAI, according to the page.

## What online stores should do now

These are Brandella's recommendations based on the current help page.

![Steps for stores to manage ChatGPT shopping results: check how products appear, clean product data, use Shopify Catalog or apply for direct feeds, keep price and stock accurate, report errors](/images/infographics/ig-chatgpt-shopping-results-partnerships-2.webp)

1. See how you appear today. Ask ChatGPT the questions your buyers ask, on web and mobile, and note which products and merchants show up and in what order.
2. Fix your product data. Structured metadata is one of the three named inputs. Clean titles, prices, availability and attributes in every feed that third party providers can read.
3. Get into the feed directly. If you sell on Shopify, OpenAI says your data is already integrated through Shopify Catalog. Other stores can apply for direct feeds access. OpenAI's [guide to submitting product feeds](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/submit-product-feeds) starts in Business Hub, where you claim the business and verify identity, then upload a full catalog by manual upload, a hosted URL or SFTP.
4. Keep price and stock accurate. Availability and price are named ranking factors for merchants, and stale data can push you down the list.
5. Make the maker or primary seller clear. If you make the product, say so plainly on product pages and in your feed.
6. Report errors. The page lets users flag products with incorrect information, so check your listings and use that route when something is wrong.
7. Track it monthly. Wording on this page has changed before and may change again, so add it to the pages you review.

## FAQ about ChatGPT shopping results

### Are ChatGPT shopping results ads?

OpenAI's help page says product results are not ads. Ads are covered in a separate help article.

### Did OpenAI confirm partnerships now affect results?

No. OpenAI removed the sentence saying results were not influenced by partnerships, but the page does not say partnerships now play a role, and we found no OpenAI statement on the change.

### How can my store appear in ChatGPT shopping results?

Shopify merchants are already included through Shopify Catalog, according to OpenAI. Other merchants can apply for direct product feed access through OpenAI's developer site.

### What decides which merchant is listed first?

OpenAI says it ranks merchants on factors including availability, price, quality and whether they are the maker or primary seller.
