<p class="bx-lede">YouTube just announced the biggest changes to the YouTube Partner Program since 2018, and they take effect February 1, 2027. If you're already earning through YPP, the headline is reassuring: your channel keeps its status. But Shorts earnings, activity rules, and your monetization terms are all changing, and there's a January 31, 2027 deadline most creators haven't noticed yet.</p>

<p><a href="https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">YouTube's own blog post</a> frames this as an investment in the program, they expect to pay creators more in 2027 than in 2026 overall. But the mechanics underneath that promise matter a lot depending on what kind of content you make and how your channel currently earns.</p>

<p>Here's the short version: new creators applying to YPP now need roughly double the watch hours or Shorts views to get in. Existing partners keep their status automatically, but Shorts revenue sharing gets a new monthly bar to clear, and everyone currently in the program has to re-accept their monetization terms before the deadline or lose earnings temporarily.</p>

<p>Let's walk through exactly what's changing, who it affects, and what to actually do about it before January 31, 2027.</p>

<h2 id="whats-changing"><span class="bx-num">01</span>What's Actually Changing, in Plain Terms</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>YouTube's own announcement, referenced above, published August 10, 2026, calls this the first significant update to the YouTube Partner Program since 2018. With over 3 million creators already in the program, the company frames these changes as necessary to keep rewarding genuinely active creators as the platform has grown.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-shorts-payout-rules/585331/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Search Engine Journal's coverage</a> of the update breaks the news into four concrete pieces: a new monthly minimum tied to Shorts earnings, updated rules for what counts as an "active" channel, a re-acceptance deadline for current terms, and doubled entry requirements for brand-new applicants.</p>

<h2 id="shorts-minimum"><span class="bx-num">03</span>Shorts Earnings Get a Monthly Minimum</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>This is the change getting the most attention, and for good reason. YouTube's own announcement, referenced above, confirms it directly: starting February 1, 2027, creators need 10 million qualified Shorts views over the last 90 days to be eligible for ads and subscription revenue sharing on Shorts specifically.</p>

<p>Fall below that threshold and you don't lose your YPP status. Google's blog is explicit that channels stay in the program and keep earning from long-form content, with Shorts revenue sharing automatically resuming the moment a channel crosses 10 million views again. It's a pause, not a removal, a detail also confirmed on <a href="https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009" target="_blank" rel="noopener">YouTube's own Help Center page</a> covering the update.</p>

<div class="bx-dyk">
<span class="lbl">🔎 Did you know?</span>
<p>YouTube says this change is unlikely to affect creators who already earn significant revenue from Shorts. The 10 million view bar is aimed at channels earning very little from Shorts anyway, redirecting that time and effort toward new incentive programs instead.</p>

<h2 id="activity-rules"><span class="bx-num">04</span>New Activity Rules for Channels Already Earning</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>Search Engine Journal's reporting, referenced above, lays out the new bar for staying "active" in the program: starting February 1, 2027, a channel counts as active if it has at least 1,000 qualified watch hours in the past year, or 1 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, or has uploaded two long-form videos or five Shorts every 90 days.</p>

<p>The old rule was much looser, just not going six months without uploading or posting, per the <a href="https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851" target="_blank" rel="noopener">current YPP overview page</a>. Channels that don't meet any of the new criteria aren't cut off immediately either. The reporting confirms there's a 90-day grace period to hit either the watch-hour or Shorts-view bar before anything changes.</p>

<h2 id="new-terms"><span class="bx-num">05</span>Current Partners Must Accept New Terms</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>This is the deadline most existing creators are likely to miss if they're not checking YouTube Studio regularly. Current partners have until January 31, 2027 to accept three separate sets of terms: the Watch Page Monetization Module, the Shorts Monetization Module, and, where it applies, the Commerce Product Module.</p>

<p>Miss the deadline and monetization from those specific features pauses on February 1, resuming once you accept the terms later. Importantly, the same coverage notes YouTube says this doesn't affect your underlying program status, it's a pause on earnings from those features, not an expulsion.</p>

<h2 id="entry-thresholds"><span class="bx-num">07</span>Entry Thresholds Double for New Creators</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>If you're not in the program yet, this is the part that affects you directly. YouTube's blog confirms new applicants will need 1,000 subscribers plus either 8,000 qualified watch hours in the past year or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, roughly double the current 4,000 hours or 10 million Shorts views.</p>

<div class="bx-tip">
<span class="lbl">💡 Quick tip</span>
<ul>
<li>Fan Funding and Shopping entry requirements are staying exactly the same, 500 subscribers and 3,000 watch hours or 3 million Shorts views, confirmed on the <a href="https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240" target="_blank" rel="noopener">expanded YPP page</a>.</li>
<li>If you're close to the old thresholds, applying before February 1, 2027 locks you into the current, lower bar.</li>
<li>New incentive programs (Shopping bonuses, brand deal support) are specifically aimed at channels below the Shorts threshold, worth watching for details.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="why-matters"><span class="bx-num">08</span>Why This Matters</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>Search Engine Journal's analysis, drawn from the same reporting, puts it plainly: this raises the bar for getting accepted into the YouTube Partner Program overall. Come February, a channel under the 10 million Shorts view mark earns nothing from the Shorts Creator Pool that specific month, while long-form earnings continue as normal.</p>

<p>The reporting flags the acceptance deadline as the thing most likely to catch creators off guard. It shows up quietly in YouTube Studio's dashboard or Earn tab, and if nobody logs into the channel regularly, it's easy to miss entirely, with real earnings consequences.</p>

<h2 id="faq"><span class="bx-num">13</span>FAQs on the YouTube Partner Program Changes</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>

<div class="bx-faq" id="bxFaq">
  <button class="bx-q">Will I lose my YouTube Partner Program status?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>No. Existing partners keep their YPP status under the new rules. What can pause is specific revenue, like Shorts earnings below the new threshold, not your overall membership.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">What happens if I miss the January 31, 2027 deadline?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>Monetization from the Watch Page, Shorts, and Commerce Product modules pauses starting February 1. It resumes once you accept the terms, and your program status isn't affected either way.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">Do I need 10 million Shorts views every single month?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>You need 10 million qualified Shorts views within a rolling 90-day window to be eligible for Shorts Creator Pool earnings that month. It recalculates continuously, not on a fixed monthly reset.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">Are the entry requirements really doubling for new creators?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>Roughly, yes. Watch hours move from 4,000 to 8,000 in the past year, and Shorts views move from 10 million to 20 million in the last 90 days.</p></div>
  <button class="bx-q">Does this affect Fan Funding or Shopping features?<span class="ic">+</span></button>
  <div class="bx-a"><p>No. YouTube confirmed the entry thresholds for Fan Funding and Shopping remain unchanged: 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads, and either 3,000 watch hours or 3 million Shorts views.</p></div>
