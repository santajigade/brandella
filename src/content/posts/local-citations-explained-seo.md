<h2 id="what-are-citations"><span class="bx-num">01</span>What Are Local Citations, Explained Simply</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/what-are-local-citations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">BrightLocal's guide to local citations</a>, a citation is any online mention of your business's name, address, and phone number, usually shortened to NAP, whether that mention lives on a directory, a review site, or a local news page.</p>

<p>A modern citation often carries a fourth element too. Industry practitioners increasingly refer to NAPW: name, address, phone number, and website, since the website link is what lets search engines connect a directory listing back to your owned domain.</p>

<p>Local citations explained this way sound simple, and they are. What trips businesses up is inconsistency, the same business showing up as "Smith & Sons Plumbing LLC" on one site and "Smith and Sons Plumbing" on another, which quietly damages the entity-matching process search engines depend on.</p>

<h2 id="why-citations-matter"><span class="bx-num">02</span>Why Local Citations Matter for SEO in 2026</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://www.rallio.com/nap-citations-local-seo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Rallio's breakdown of the 2026 Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors study</a>, citation signals account for roughly 7% of the ranking weight inside Google's local pack algorithm, a smaller slice than Google Business Profile optimization but still a mandatory trust foundation.</p>

<p>That last point is the one people miss. Without accurate citations, your other local SEO work doesn't reach its full potential, because Google's algorithm uses citations to corroborate the claims your Google Business Profile and website are already making.</p>

<p>Citations feed directly into two of Google's three core local ranking pillars. They strengthen relevance by confirming what your business does, and they strengthen prominence by proving your business is genuinely well-known across the web, not just self-reported in one place.</p>

<h2 id="structured-unstructured"><span class="bx-num">03</span>Structured vs Unstructured Local Citations Explained</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://www.semrush.com/blog/what-are-local-citations-in-seo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Semrush's overview of local citations</a>, citations come in two distinct forms, and most businesses only ever build one of them.</p>

<p>Structured citations are formal directory listings, think Google Business Profile, Yelp, Facebook, and Bing Places, where your NAP sits in a dedicated field. Unstructured citations are organic mentions buried inside blog posts, news articles, or local sponsorship pages, where your business details appear naturally in the content.</p>

<p>According to <a href="https://www.rankiwis.com/nap-citations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Rankiwis' 2026 guide to NAP citations</a>, structured citations are now table stakes since nearly every serious local business already has them. What differentiates competitive citation profiles today is unstructured citations, genuine editorial mentions that can't be automated or bought in bulk.</p>

<h2 id="nap-consistency"><span class="bx-num">04</span>NAP Consistency Is the Real Ranking Lever</h2>
<div class="bx-rule"></div>
<p>According to <a href="https://www.webtonic.io/blog/nap-seo-why-name-address-phone-consistency-matters-for-local-rankings-in-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Webtonic's 2026 research on NAP consistency</a>, businesses with consistent NAP data across major citation sources are 40% more likely to appear in the Google Local Pack, the three-listing block that captures the majority of local clicks.</p>

<p>The same research found that after businesses removed duplicate listings and corrected inconsistencies, 68% saw ranking improvements within 30 to 60 days, with an average position lift of 2.3 spots in local pack results.</p>

<div class="bx-checklist">
<div class="bx-check-item"><div class="bx-check-icon"></div><p><strong>Audit your core citations first</strong>: Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, and Facebook.</p></div>
<div class="bx-check-item"><div class="bx-check-icon"></div><p><strong>Fix data aggregator records</strong> like Data Axle and Localeze, since errors there get pushed back out to dozens of downstream sites.</p></div>
<div class="bx-check-item"><div class="bx-check-icon"></div><p><strong>Standardize formatting once</strong>, then reuse the exact same NAP string everywhere, including abbreviations like "St." vs "Street."</p></div>
<div class="bx-check-item"><div class="bx-check-icon"></div><p><strong>Re-audit quarterly</strong>, since a move, phone change, or rebrand can silently erode rankings within months.</p></div>
</div>

<h2 id="aggregators"><span class="bx-num">05</span>How Data Aggregators Feed Your Citations</h2>
